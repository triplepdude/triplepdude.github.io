It halved a screen recording but saved only 2% on the test clip, where the picture moves and almost every pixel changes. ## Which frame rate to use GIF frame times are whole hundredths of a second, so 5, 10, 20 and 25 fps play exactly. At 30 fps the frames take 3, 4 and 3 hundredths in turn: the tool spreads the rounding so the total length stays right. Browsers play a frame time of one hundredth or less as a tenth of a second, so no GIF plays faster than 50 fps. 10 to 15 fps suits reactions and memes; 20 to 30 fps looks smooth but doubles or triples the size. **Speed** changes how much video each frame covers, not the frame times: at 2×, a 6-second part of the video becomes a 3-second GIF. ## Why GIFs look grainy Each GIF frame holds at most 256 colours. The tool picks one palette from frames across your selection, and **dithering** mixes neighbouring colours in a fine, fixed pattern so that skies and skin do not break into bands. Close up the pattern looks like grain, but it stays still from frame to frame instead of shimmering, and colours the palette holds exactly, such as the flat background of a screen recording, stay clean. Without dithering, gradients turn into stripes. ## Videos that will not open AVI, WMV and FLV files do not play in browsers: convert them to MP4 first. iPhone videos in HEVC (the High Efficiency setting) open in Safari, but in Chrome, Edge or Firefox on Windows and Linux only when the computer can decode HEVC.
