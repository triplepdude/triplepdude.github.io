Tags that spell anything else are removed. - **Scripts that need joiners.** A zero-width non-joiner or joiner between two letters of Persian, Arabic, Hindi, Bengali and similar scripts changes how the word is written, so it stays. Between Latin letters it goes. - **Old-style Hangul syllables** built from conjoining jamo keep a filler that stands in for a missing part. - **Visible format characters**, such as the Arabic number signs U+0600 to U+0605, are left alone. Collapsing spaces keeps the indentation at the start of a line, and tabs are removed only at the end of a line. ## How em dashes are replaced With **comma**, "the result—surprisingly—was good" becomes "the result, surprisingly, was good". A dash between two numbers becomes a hyphen (1990-2000), a dash right before other punctuation such as a full stop or closing bracket is dropped, and a dash at the start of a line becomes a hyphen. **Hyphen with spaces** puts one space on each side of the hyphen (except at the start or end of a line), and **plain hyphen** swaps the dash for `-` and keeps the spacing. Read the result once: an em dash sometimes works as a colon, and no rule can know that. ## Hidden characters and AI detectors Invisible characters can come from typography (a narrow no-break space before a unit), from the app you copied from, or from deliberate tampering. Tag characters mirror printable ASCII and have been used to hide instructions in text given to AI tools; the highlighted view decodes them so you can read what was hidden.
