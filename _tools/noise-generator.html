White noise has the same power at every frequency, so the top octave, 10 to 20 kHz, holds half of it. Pink noise falls by 3 dB per octave, so every octave has the same power. Green noise has no standard definition. Sleep sites use the name for noise concentrated around 500 Hz. Here it is pink noise through a broad 500 Hz band-pass filter, rolling off the deep bass and high treble. Grey noise follows the ISO 226 40-phon equal-loudness contour, louder where the ear is less sensitive, so every pitch sounds about as loud. Following it exactly would boost 20 Hz by 60 dB, so the bass boost stops at 20 dB. ## Sleep timer Choose 15 minutes to 8 hours, or a clock time. In a 2026 laboratory study of 25 adults, pink noise at 50 dBA cut REM sleep by about 19 minutes a night, and foam earplugs protected sleep from environmental noise better than pink noise did. If you use noise to cover other sounds, choose the colour you find least distracting, keep it quiet and let the timer stop it." - q: Is white noise safe for babies? a: Keep it quiet, far away and short. When 14 infant sleep machines were measured at full volume (Pediatrics, 2014), all were louder than 50 dBA at 30 cm, the limit recommended for hospital nurseries, and three exceeded 85 dBA. Put the phone or speaker as far from the cot as practical, never in or on it, use a low volume and set the timer to stop the noise once your baby is asleep. A 2026 study in which pink noise reduced REM sleep in adults also cautions against using such noise indiscriminately with newborns and toddlers. - q: Does brown noise help with ADHD? a: "Nobody has shown that it does: a 2024 meta-analysis of noise and ADHD found no study of brown noise at all. Across 13 studies, white or pink noise slightly improved performance on laboratory attention tasks in children and young adults with ADHD or many ADHD symptoms, and slightly worsened it in those without. Noise is not a treatment for ADHD, so talk to a doctor about care." - q: Can white noise help with tinnitus? a: It may make it less noticeable.
