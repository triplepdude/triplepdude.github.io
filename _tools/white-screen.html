Runs in your browser. category: Device Tests keywords: [white screen, bright white screen, blank white screen, white screen online, full screen white, black screen, white light screen, screen light for video calls, color screen] howto: | ## How to use the white screen 1. Pick a colour. **White** comes first; **Custom** takes any colour from the picker or a code such as `#FF8800`. 2. Set the **colour temperature** of white and the **brightness** of any colour. 3. Press **Full screen**, click the preview, or focus it and press **F**. Move the mouse or tap to bring up the controls. In full screen, ← and → change the colour, ↑ and ↓ the brightness, and Esc takes you back. Your choices are kept in the page address, so a bookmark opens the same screen again. **Download PNG** saves the colour as an image the size of your screen. On an iPhone, where Safari cannot show a web page in full screen, save that image to Photos and view it there. ## Colour temperature | Setting | Looks like | |---|---| | 1900 K | Candle flame | | 2700 K | Warm white bulb | | 4000 K | Neutral white | | 5000 K | D50, the standard light for judging prints | | 6500 K | D65, average daylight and the white of sRGB screens | | 10000 K | Bluish white | Below 4000 K the colour follows the glow of a heated filament (the Planckian locus); from 4000 K up, the CIE daylight series. 6500 K is plain `#FFFFFF`, your screen's own white. 1900 K is the warmest white an sRGB screen can show: lower temperatures lie outside its range of colours. Night Shift, Night Light and True Tone tint everything on the screen, this page included, so turn them off for a neutral white. ## Brightness A web page cannot change your screen's brightness setting, so the slider dims the colour itself. It works in light output: 50% gives about half the light, which is `#BCBCBC` for white, not `#808080`, because screens do not respond to colour codes in a straight line. For the most light, set the device to full brightness, switch off automatic brightness and leave the slider at 100%. ## What a white screen is good for - **Video calls:** a bright screen in front of you softens shadows on your face. A 13-inch laptop screen at 400 cd/m² gives off at most about 65 lumens, against about 800 from a 60 W bulb, so it helps close up in a dim room. Match the room's light, warm for lamps and cooler by a window, and check the effect with the [webcam test](/webcam-test/). - **Tracing:** lay a tablet flat at full brightness, tape the paper on top and trace. On an iPhone or iPad, Guided Access with Touch switched off ignores your hand and pencil. - **Cleaning:** show **Black** in a lit room. Dust, smears and fingerprints catch the light and are easy to see. - **Faulty pixels:** a solid colour shows dark or bright dots, but the [dead pixel test](/dead-pixel-test/) steps through every test colour and explains what each fault looks like. faq: - q: Does this page use my camera or store anything? a: No. It only fills the screen with a colour. It never asks for the camera or microphone, and nothing is uploaded or saved in your browser; your settings live only in the page address. - q: Is a white screen a real light source? a: Yes. A full white makes every pixel give out as much light as your brightness setting allows. OLED screens, TVs above all, often dim a full-screen white to limit power and heat, so it can look less bright than a small white window. - q: How do I exit full screen? a: Press Esc, or tap the screen and choose Exit. With the colour in focus, F also switches full screen on and off. If you used your browser's own full-screen key, such as F11, press it again; on a phone the back gesture works too. - q: Why does my white look yellow or blue? a: At 6500 K this page shows `#FFFFFF`, so any tint comes from the screen. Night Shift, Night Light, True Tone, eye-comfort modes and the monitor's own colour preset all change its white. Turn them off, or pick the warmth you want with the colour temperature slider. ---
