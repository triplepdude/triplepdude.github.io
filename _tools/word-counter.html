--- title: Word Counter description: Count words, characters, sentences, paragraphs, and reading time as you type. Free, instant, and private. Your text never leaves your browser. category: Text keywords: [word count, character count, character counter, letter counter, reading time, sentence counter] howto: | ## How to use the word counter Type or paste your text into the box. Every count updates as you type, so you can watch an essay, a post, or a meta description stay within its limit. Use **Copy text** to copy everything back out, or **Clear** to start over. ## How each number is counted - **Words.** A word is a run of letters, digits, or accent marks. Apostrophes and hyphens inside a word keep it together, so *don't* and *well-known* are one word each. Numbers written with a decimal point, thousands separator, or colon, such as *3.5*, *1,000*, and *10:30*, also count as one word. Punctuation and symbols such as *&*, *—*, or emoji are not words. - **Chinese and Japanese.** These languages don't put spaces between words, so each Han, Hiragana, or Katakana character counts as one word. Word processors use the same convention. Korean uses spaces, so it is counted like English. - **Characters** counts what a reader sees as one character. An emoji with a skin tone, a flag, or a letter with a separate accent mark is stored as several code points but counts once. Spaces and line breaks are included. **Characters (no spaces)** leaves out all whitespace. - **Sentences** end at a period, question mark, exclamation mark, or ellipsis, including the full-width 。！？ used in Chinese and Japanese. Abbreviations like *e.g.* or *Dr.* end a sentence too, so treat the sentence count as a close estimate. - **Paragraphs** are blocks of text separated by at least one blank line. ## Reading and speaking time Reading time uses **238 words per minute**, the average silent reading speed for adults reading non-fiction in English, from a 2019 meta-analysis of 190 studies by Marc Brysbaert. Speaking time uses **150 words per minute**, a typical pace for a presentation or a podcast. Your own speed may differ, so use them as estimates when planning a talk or a blog post. ## Common length limits | Where | Limit | |---|---| | X (Twitter) post | 280 characters | | Google search result title | about 60 characters before it is cut off | | Meta description | about 155 characters before it is cut off | | SMS message | 160 characters, or 70 if it contains emoji or other non-GSM characters | | Common App personal essay | 650 words | Search engines truncate titles and descriptions by pixel width rather than a fixed character count, so the numbers above are guides, not hard limits. faq: - q: Is my text uploaded anywhere? a: No. The counting happens entirely in your browser with JavaScript. Nothing you type is sent to a server or stored. - q: Why might my word processor show a slightly different count? a: Programs disagree on edge cases such as words joined by a dash without spaces, URLs, and symbols. This counter's rules are listed above so you know exactly what it counts. - q: Does it count emoji and accented characters correctly? a: Yes. Characters are counted as user-perceived characters, so an emoji or an accented letter counts as one character even when it is stored as several code points. - q: How long does it take to read 1,000 words? a: About 4 minutes 12 seconds at the average adult reading speed of 238 words per minute, and about 6 minutes 40 seconds to read aloud at 150 words per minute. ---
