Fill in your business details, add a logo if you like, and enter who you are billing. 2. Check the invoice number and dates. **Payment terms** set the due date: *Net 30* means 30 days after the issue date. 3. Add a line for each product or service with its quantity and rate. The preview updates as you type and is exactly what goes into the PDF. ## How the totals are calculated Money is counted in whole minor units (cents, pence, fils), never in floating-point numbers, so the rounding errors that make some tools show 1.005 × 1 as 1.00 cannot happen here. Type amounts with one decimal point or comma and no thousands separators, so 1,234 is never ambiguous. - **Each line** is quantity × rate, rounded to the currency's smallest unit. A half rounds away from zero, so 49.995 becomes 50.00. - **Subtotal** is the sum of the lines. - **Discount** is a percentage of the subtotal, rounded once, or a fixed amount. - **Each tax** is its rate times the subtotal after the discount, plus shipping when *Charge tax on shipping* is ticked. It is rounded once on that total rather than line by line. - **Total** is subtotal − discount + shipping + taxes, and **balance due** is the total minus the amount paid. The number of decimals follows the currency. It comes from the currency data built into your browser, which matches ISO 4217 except for a few currencies whose minor unit is rarely used, such as the Iraqi and Serbian dinars, which get no decimals: | Currency | Decimals | Example | |---|---|---| | US dollar, euro, pound | 2 | 1,234.56 | | Japanese yen, Korean won | 0 | 1,235 | | Kuwaiti dinar, Bahraini dinar, Omani rial | 3 | 1,234.567 | A rate may have more decimals than the currency, such as 0.0125 per unit; the line amount is still rounded to the currency. ## Why the PDF comes from the print dialog The PDF is made by your browser's own print engine. It uses the fonts on your device, so names and descriptions in any script, including Chinese, Arabic, Hindi and Cyrillic, are shaped correctly, the text stays selectable and searchable, and the file stays small. A JavaScript PDF library such as pdf-lib needs a font file embedded for every script and does no text shaping, so Arabic letters would not join and Hindi would come out garbled. In the dialog, pick *Save as PDF* in Chrome, Edge or on Android, *Save to PDF* in Firefox, or open the *PDF* menu and choose *Save as PDF* in Safari on a Mac. ## Saving and reusing invoices Nothing is kept in your browser, so save the invoice file (a small .json file) with your records. Opening it restores every field, including the logo. **Next invoice** keeps your business details, logo, currency, taxes, notes and terms, clears the client and items, and increases the number, so INV-0009 becomes INV-0010. ## What to put on an invoice Most tax authorities expect a unique, sequential invoice number, the issue date, your name and address, the customer's name and address, a description, quantity and price for each item, and the tax rate and tax amount. In the EU and the UK, a VAT invoice must also show your VAT registration number, so put it in the Tax ID field. Rules differ between countries, so check what yours requires. faq: - q: Is my invoice data uploaded or stored? a: No. The invoice is built by JavaScript in your browser, and the PDF is made by your browser's print function.
