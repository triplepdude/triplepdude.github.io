Copy or download the log before you close the page. ## What is the Pomodoro Technique? Francesco Cirillo worked it out in the late 1980s as a university student, timing his study with a kitchen timer shaped like a tomato (pomodoro in Italian). Choose one task, work on it for 25 minutes, then take a break of 3 to 5 minutes; after four sessions, take 15 to 30. A session cannot be split: if an interruption ends it, it does not count and you start a new one. He wrote that sessions of 20 to 35 minutes work best, 40 at most, so 25 is a starting point rather than a rule. Nobody has shown that. 52/17 comes from 2014 data on the most productive users of the time tracker DeskTime, which found 112/26 in 2021; neither was peer reviewed, and 50/10 has no study behind it. Two small trials at Maastricht University compared fixed breaks with breaks students chose themselves. In one, with 87 students, those on fixed breaks felt less tired and more focused, and got as much done in less time; in the other, with 94 students, tiredness rose faster on 25/5, but overall tiredness and the work done did not differ. So choose the length you can keep up. ## What should I do in a break, and how many sessions a day? Cirillo suggests standing up, walking about or having a drink of water, and keeping away from work talk and email. The NHS advises breaking up long periods of sitting, and the American Academy of Ophthalmology's 20-20-20 rule is to look at something 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes. For sessions, Cirillo suggests starting with one uninterrupted session a day and building up to 10 to 12. ## Does it keep running in another tab or on a locked phone? In another tab, yes: each end sound, and the next session's when that starts by itself, is scheduled ahead on the sound card's clock, which keeps time while the browser slows down background tabs. With ticking on, the tab plays sound, so Chrome neither slows nor discards it. Edge on battery may put a quiet background tab to sleep after 5 minutes: add this site under "Always keep these sites active" in its Performance settings. On a phone keep the page in front: Safari on an iPhone pauses it when the screen locks or you switch apps, and Chrome on Android freezes a hidden tab after 5 minutes. The timer cannot block websites, ring from a closed tab or keep your history.
