The page address keeps the colour, so you can bookmark it or send the link. ## Converting HEX to RGB by hand A HEX code is three pairs of base-16 digits: red, green and blue. The digits 0 to 9 count as themselves and A to F as 10 to 15. Multiply the first digit of a pair by 16 and add the second: | Pair | Working | Value | |---|---|---| | FF | 15 × 16 + 15 | 255 | | 57 | 5 × 16 + 7 | 87 | | 33 | 3 × 16 + 3 | 51 | So `#FF5733` is `rgb(255, 87, 51)`. To go the other way, divide by 16: 87 ÷ 16 is 5 remainder 7, so 87 is `57`. A three-digit code doubles each digit, so `#F53` is `#FF5533`. The case of the letters does not matter. For other base-16 numbers, use the [binary translator](/binary-translator/). ## 8-digit HEX and transparency `#RRGGBBAA` adds a fourth pair for alpha: `00` is fully transparent and `FF` fully opaque. `80` is 128 ÷ 255, about 50%. Browsers have read it since Chrome 62, Firefox 49, Safari 10 and Edge 79. Android puts alpha first instead, as `#AARRGGBB`; the ARGB line gives that order, and the converter reads `0x` with eight digits that way too. ## HSL, HWB and OKLCH HSL gives a hue angle (0 is red, 120 green, 240 blue), a saturation and a lightness. Keeping the hue and changing the lightness gives lighter and darker shades of one colour, a common use in CSS. Its lightness does not match what the eye sees, though: yellow and blue at 50% look very different. OKLCH, supported since Chrome 111, Firefox 113 and Safari 15.4, is designed so that equal steps in lightness look about equal. It can also describe colours that HEX cannot hold; such a colour is brought into range by lowering its chroma at the same lightness and hue, as in the CSS Color 4 specification. ## CMYK is only a rough guide The CMYK line uses the simple formula K = 1 − max(R, G, B) and C = (1 − R − K) ÷ (1 − K), the same for M and Y. Real print colours depend on the inks, the paper and the press, so design software converts with the printer's ICC colour profile, and very bright screen colours cannot be printed at all. Ask your printer for their profile before sending files. ## Contrast for text WCAG 2.2 asks for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (18 point, about 24 px, or 14 point bold, about 18.5 px) at level AA, and 7:1 and 4.5:1 at level AAA. Ratios are not rounded, so 4.499:1 does not meet 4.5:1; the converter cuts them to two decimals.
