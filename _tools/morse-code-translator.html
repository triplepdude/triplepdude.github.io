Speed, tone, separators and dot and dash symbols are all adjustable. ## Writing Morse code by hand Use `.` for a dot and `-` for a dash (`·` `•` `−` `▬` and `_` work too). Put one space between letters and `/` or `|` between words; a gap of two or more spaces also counts as a word break, and line breaks are kept. Codes that are not Morse show as `#`. Prosigns, which are sent as one signal without letter gaps, go in angle brackets: ``, ``, ``. ## Timing and words per minute | Element | Length | |---|---| | Dot (dit) | 1 unit | | Dash (dah) | 3 units | | Gap inside a character | 1 unit | | Gap between letters | 3 units | | Gap between words | 7 units | Speed is measured with the word PARIS, which takes exactly 50 units including the gap after it. At *w* words per minute one unit therefore lasts 1200 ÷ *w* milliseconds: 60 ms at 20 WPM, 100 ms at 12 WPM. The timing line under the controls shows every length for your settings. ## Farnsworth spacing Farnsworth timing sends each character at the full character speed but stretches the gaps between letters and words, so the overall speed is lower. Learners hear every letter as a rhythm instead of counting dots. The page uses the formula from the ARRL's Farnsworth timing standard: with character speed *c* and overall speed *s*, each word gets (60*c* − 37.2*s*) ÷ (*s* × *c*) seconds of extra delay, of which 3/19 goes into each gap between letters and 7/19 into the gap between words. At 20 WPM characters and 10 WPM overall, a letter gap is 654 ms and a word gap 1,525 ms. ## The ITU standard and extras The code follows ITU-R M.1677-1: the letters A to Z plus É, the digits, and the signs `. , : ? ' - / ( ) " = + × @`. It also defines service signals, which the translator writes as prosigns: understood `...-.` ``, error `........` ``, wait `.-...` ``, end of work `...-.-` ``, and starting signal `-.-.-` ``. The ITU sends % as 0/0, and this page does the same. The multiplication sign × is the same code as X. Signs such as `! & ; $ _` and letters like Ä, Ñ, Ö and Ü are not in the ITU standard but are widely used; they are marked with an asterisk in the chart. Untick **Common non-ITU signs** to leave them out. Other accented letters are sent as their base letter, so ô becomes O. ## SOS The distress signal is sent as one continuous sequence, `...---...`, with no gaps between the letters.
