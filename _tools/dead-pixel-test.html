--- title: "Dead Pixel Test: Check Your Screen Online" description: Find dead, stuck and hot pixels with full-screen solid colours, then check backlight bleed and uniformity with gradients and a checkerboard. category: Device Tests keywords: [dead pixel test, stuck pixel test, screen test, monitor test, backlight bleed test, screen color test, dead pixel checker] howto: | ## How to test for dead pixels 1. Wipe the screen with a dry microfibre cloth, so dust isn't mistaken for a faulty pixel. 2. Press **Start full-screen test**. Each pattern fills the whole screen. 3. Step through the colours with the arrow keys or Space, or click or tap the screen (the left third goes back). Press Esc to finish. 4. Scan the whole screen on every colour from your normal viewing distance, then look closely at anything suspicious. On a dense screen a magnifying glass or your phone camera's zoom helps. The controls fade after two seconds and the mouse pointer hides, so neither can be mistaken for a fault. Tick **Auto-cycle** to step through the patterns by themselves; they change no faster than once a second, well below the three flashes per second that accessibility guidelines treat as a seizure risk. The screen is kept awake during the test where the browser allows it. ## Dead, stuck and hot pixels Every pixel is made of red, green and blue sub-pixels, and each kind of fault shows up best on a different background: | Fault | What you see | Where to look | Fixable at home? | |---|---|---|---| | Dead pixel | A black dot | White and bright colours | Rarely | | Stuck pixel | A red, green, blue or mixed dot | Black and the other colours | Sometimes | | Hot pixel | A white dot | Black | Rarely | | Dead sub-pixel | A dark dot on one colour only | Red, green and blue | Rarely | On an LCD the result of a failed transistor depends on the panel. IPS and VA panels are *normally black*, so the pixel stays dark; TN panels are normally white, so it stays lit. On an OLED each sub-pixel makes its own light, and a dead one is simply dark. ## Can a stuck pixel be fixed? A stuck sub-pixel is still wired up but frozen in one state. Running a rapidly changing colour pattern over it for 10 to 30 minutes, often called pixel exercising, sometimes frees it; there is no guarantee, and fast flashing is unsuitable for anyone with photosensitive epilepsy. Pressing gently on the spot through a soft cloth is another old trick, but pressure can damage the panel, so treat it as a last resort. Dead and hot pixels usually come from a failed transistor or connection and cannot be repaired at home. ## Backlight bleed and uniformity Show **Black** in a dark room with brightness at your usual setting. Light leaking in from the edges that stays put when you move your head is backlight bleed. A silvery glow in the corners that changes with your viewing angle is IPS glow, which is normal for IPS panels. **Grey 50%** and **Dark grey 5%** reveal clouding, darker corners and tinted patches, and near-black banding on OLEDs. The **gradients** should look smooth: clear steps are banding, common on 6-bit panels and with some colour settings. On the **checkerboard**, white squares should be equally bright everywhere without glowing into their black neighbours. Phone cameras exaggerate bleed, so judge with your eyes. ## Pixel fault policies The ISO 13406-2 standard, since replaced by ISO 9241-307 but still quoted by many manufacturers, allowed a Class II panel up to 2 always-lit and 2 always-dark pixels plus 5 faulty sub-pixels per million pixels. A 1920 × 1080 screen has about 2.1 million pixels, so a few faults could still pass. Many brands publish stricter zero bright dot policies, and a retailer's return window is often the simplest remedy, so test a new screen straight away. faq: - q: Does this test upload or record anything? a: No. The page only draws colours in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, recorded or stored. - q: How do I get out of full screen? a: Press Esc, or click or tap the screen and choose Exit on the bar at the bottom. On a phone you can also use the back gesture. - q: Is it a dead pixel or a speck of dust? a: Dust sits on the surface, so it often looks slightly blurred, casts a faint shadow and moves or disappears when you wipe the screen. A pixel fault is perfectly sharp, stays in place, and is exactly one pixel or sub-pixel in size. - q: Does it work on phones, tablets and TVs? a: Yes. Tap to change colours on a touch screen. iPhones do not let web pages go full screen, so there the test fills the browser window instead. On a smart TV, open the page in its browser, or connect a computer and run it full screen. ---
