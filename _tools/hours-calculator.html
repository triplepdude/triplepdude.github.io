Type them the way you would say them: `9`, `9:30`, `9:30 pm`, `930p`, `21:30`, `noon` and `midnight` all work, and the field is tidied into your chosen 12-hour or 24-hour format when you move on. Add a row for every shift or time block, such as each day of a week, and the total at the bottom adds them all up. Put **unpaid** break time in the break column, in minutes. In the United States, the federal rules generally treat short rest breaks of 5 to 20 minutes as paid work time, while a real meal break, usually 30 minutes or more with no duties, can be unpaid (29 CFR 785.18 and 785.19). Leave paid breaks out. Add an hourly rate to see gross pay before tax. Pay is worked out from the exact minutes, not from the rounded decimal, so 8:20 at 15.00 an hour pays 125.00, while 8.33 × 15 would give 124.95. For overtime, use the [time card calculator](/time-card-calculator/). ## Overnight shifts When the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator assumes the shift ends the next day: 10:00 PM to 6:30 AM is 8 hours 30 minutes. A row with the same start and end counts as zero, so enter `24:00` as the end for a full 24-hour shift. Split anything longer than 24 hours into two rows. ## Converting minutes to decimal hours Payroll and invoicing systems usually want decimal hours. Divide the minutes by 60 and add them to the whole hours: 7:45 is 7 + 45 ÷ 60 = **7.75** hours, and 8:20 is 8.3333… hours, which rounds to 8.33. US federal rules allow rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour, as long as it averages out over time and does not end up underpaying employees (29 CFR 785.48).
