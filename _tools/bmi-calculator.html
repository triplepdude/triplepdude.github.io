Runs in your browser. category: Calculators keywords: [bmi calculator, bmi chart, bmi calculator female, how do i calculate bmi, bmi for age calculator, bmi calculator teenager, bmi percentile calculator, child bmi calculator, healthy weight for height] howto: | ## How to calculate BMI BMI (body mass index) is weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres: 70 kg at 1.75 m is 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9. In pounds and inches, multiply by 703: 154 lb at 5 ft 9 in (69 in) is 703 × 154 ÷ (69 × 69) = 22.7. The calculator converts with the exact factors (1 lb is 0.45359237 kg and 1 in is 2.54 cm), so the 703 shortcut can differ in the last decimal, and rounds the BMI to one decimal before choosing the category, as the CDC does. A comma works as a decimal point, and a height in metres typed in the cm box, such as 1.75, is read as 175 cm. | Adult BMI | Category | |---|---| | Below 18.5 | Underweight | | 18.5 to 24.9 | Healthy weight | | 25 to 29.9 | Overweight | | 30 to 34.9 | Obesity, class 1 | | 35 to 39.9 | Obesity, class 2 | | 40 or more | Obesity, class 3 (severe obesity) | These are the CDC's categories for adults of 20 and over. The WHO also defines overweight as a BMI of 25 or more and obesity as 30 or more. For people with a South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family background, NICE (UK) uses lower thresholds: overweight from 23 and obesity from 27.5, with classes 2 and 3 from 32.5 and 37.5. Tick the box under the age to use them. ## What is a healthy weight for my height? Any weight that gives a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9. For 5 ft 9 in that is 125 to 168 lb, the CDC's own example, and the chart under the calculator gives the range for other heights in your units. ## BMI for children and teens From 2 to 19, BMI is compared with children of the same sex and age in the CDC growth charts, so the result is a percentile. Age counts to the month, so give the months or the dates of birth and measurement; to count months between two dates, use the [age calculator](/age-calculator/). | Percentile for age and sex | Category | |---|---| | Below the 5th | Underweight | | 5th to below 85th | Healthy weight | | 85th to below 95th | Overweight | | 95th or above | Obesity | | 120% of the 95th or more, or BMI 35 or more | Severe obesity | Above the 95th percentile the page uses the CDC's 2022 extended charts and also gives the BMI as a percentage of the 95th percentile, the "X is what percent of Y" sum of the [percentage calculator](/percentage-calculator/). The American Academy of Pediatrics calls 120% to 139% (or a BMI of 35 to 39.9) class 2 severe obesity and 140% or more (or a BMI of 40 or more) class 3. No target weight is given for a child. At 18 and 19 you also see the adult category, which the NHS uses from 18. Under 2, the CDC recommends the WHO growth standards instead, so no BMI is shown. ## What BMI cannot tell you BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis (CDC). It cannot tell muscle from fat, and NICE advises caution in interpreting it for people with a lot of muscle and for people aged 65 and over. NICE's other check for adults with a BMI under 35, and for children from 5, is the waist-to-height ratio: 0.4 to 0.49 (a waist under half your height) means no increased health risk, 0.5 to 0.59 an increased risk and 0.6 or more a further increased risk. Enter your waist to see yours. The NHS adult BMI calculator is not for use in pregnancy, and neither is this one. If you are worried about your weight or eating, talk to a doctor; the NHS page on [eating disorders](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/behaviours/eating-disorders/overview/) lists where to get help. faq: - q: Is BMI different for women? a: Not for adults. The CDC uses the same categories for everyone of 20 and over, whatever their age, sex or race. For children and teenagers the BMI is compared with others of the same sex and age, which is why the calculator asks for sex only under 20. - q: What does a child's BMI percentile mean? a: It shows how the child's BMI compares with children of the same sex and age in the CDC reference data. In the CDC's own example, a 12-year-old girl at the 96th percentile has a BMI the same as or higher than that of 96% of girls her age, which is in the obesity range. It is a screening measure, not a target or a diagnosis, so ask a doctor what it means for your child. - q: Is BMI accurate for muscular, older or Asian people? a: Less than for most people. BMI cannot distinguish fat from lean mass such as muscle (CDC), and NICE advises caution in reading it for people with a lot of muscle and for people aged 65 and over. The American Medical Association (2023) says BMI does not account for differences across race and ethnic groups, sexes, genders and ages. In the UK, NICE uses lower thresholds for people with a South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family background, which the box under the age switches on. - q: Why does my GP's chart give a different result for my child? a: The CDC charts used here are a US reference. In the UK, GPs and the NHS child calculator use the UK growth charts (UK-WHO to age 4, UK90 after), and NICE puts overweight at the 91st centile, clinical obesity at the 98th and severe obesity at the 99.6th, so the same child can land in a different group. - q: Is my height, weight or age sent anywhere? a: No. Everything is worked out in your browser and nothing is uploaded, saved or put in a cookie, or in the page address.
