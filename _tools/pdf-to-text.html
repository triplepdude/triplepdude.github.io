Press **Choose a PDF file**, or drop a PDF on the box. The text of every page appears in a moment: a 142-page tax guide takes a few seconds. 2. Choose how the text is laid out, and type **Pages** such as `1-3, 5` to keep only some of them. 3. Press **Copy text** or **Download .txt**. The file is plain UTF-8 text, which any text editor opens. On most phones, **Share** sends it to another app. ## Paragraphs, lines or layout | Text as | What you get | Good for | |---|---|---| | Paragraphs | Each paragraph on one line, a blank line between them | Documents, translation, reading aloud | | Lines as in the PDF | Every line as it is on the page | Addresses, poems, lists | | Layout | Columns and tables spaced as on the page | Tables, forms, statements | All three close up headings set with letter spacing (*F I S C A L* becomes *FISCAL*) and turn ligatures such as *ﬁ* into letters. Paragraphs also joins words split by a hyphen at a line end, which joins a compound broken at its own hyphen too, so *well-known* can become *wellknown*: untick the option for text full of compounds. In Paragraphs and Lines the cells of a table are separated by a tab, so a table pasted into a spreadsheet keeps its columns. Running heads, footers and page numbers that repeat from page to page are left out. **Between pages** puts a blank line, a `--- Page 2 ---` marker or a form feed between pages, the character pdftotext writes there. The values of filled-in form fields can be added after the text of their page. ## Why columns come out mixed up The text is taken in the order the PDF draws it, which in most documents is the reading order: one column, then the next. A paragraph that runs on into the next column or page is joined again. Magazines and pages with sidebars sometimes draw their text in another order; Layout then puts each line where it is on the page. ## Why is the text garbled? Some PDFs use fonts that do not say which letter each shape is. Their text comes out as symbols or as �, the replacement character, and the page says which pages are affected. A PDF viewer copies such text the same way, because the letters are not in the file. OCR reads them from the picture of the page, and the page offers it where much of a page is garbled. ## Scanned PDFs A scan holds pictures of pages, not text. Pages without text are listed, and the page can read them with OCR: Tesseract, the open-source OCR engine, running inside your browser. The first time, about 4.5 MB for English is downloaded from this site, and a dense page takes about ten seconds on a computer. Check what OCR reads, above all names and numbers. For photos and screenshots, use [Image to Text](/image-to-text/). ## Locked PDFs A PDF that needs a password to open asks for it, and the password stays on your device. When the author of a PDF has forbidden copying its text, this page extracts it only with the file's permissions password, or when that password is blank, as [Unlock PDF](/unlock-pdf/) does.
