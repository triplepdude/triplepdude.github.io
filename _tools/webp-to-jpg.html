A high setting keeps that second loss small, which is why 90 is the default. Going up to 100 seldom shows a visible difference yet makes the file much larger. Lower values shrink the file further, and blockiness appears first around sharp edges and in smooth skies. If you must stay under a size limit such as 200 KB, the [image compressor](/compress-image/) finds a suitable setting for you. ## Expect a bigger file WebP was designed to beat JPG on size: Google's own comparison found lossy WebP files 25 to 34% smaller than JPEGs of equal measured quality. With a handful of sample photos, a JPG at 90% weighed roughly twice as much as the same photo saved as WebP at 80%. So do not be surprised when the list reports something like "(95% larger)". The extra bytes add no detail; they are simply the cost of the older, more widely accepted format. ## Animated WebP and other formats A JPG is a single still image, so an animated WebP gives its first frame.
