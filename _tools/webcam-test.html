--- title: "Webcam Test: Check Your Camera Online" description: Test your webcam online with a live preview, real resolution and measured FPS, a check of supported resolutions, test clips and PNG snapshots. category: Device Tests keywords: [webcam test, camera test, test my webcam, webcam resolution test, webcam fps test, online camera test, webcam mirror] howto: | ## How to test your webcam 1. Pick a camera and, if you like, a resolution and frame rate, then press **Start**. Allow camera access when the browser asks. 2. The preview appears with the delivered resolution and frame rate in the corner. The figures below it update every half second. 3. **Take snapshot** saves a full-resolution PNG. **Record clip** captures 5, 10 or 30 seconds, with sound if you tick **Include microphone**, to play back and download (WebM, or MP4 where the browser cannot record WebM). 4. Press **Stop** to release the camera. The webcam light should go out a moment later; if it stays on, another app or tab is still using it. Camera names only appear once you have granted access, because browsers hide device labels from pages without permission. ## What the numbers mean **Resolution**, **Frame rate** and **Aspect ratio** come from `MediaStreamTrack.getSettings()`, which reports the mode the camera is actually running in, not the maximum printed on the box. **Measured FPS** counts the frames the page really displays. If it is well below the reported rate, the camera is dropping frames, usually because of low light: auto-exposure lengthens each exposure, and a 1/15 s exposure cannot deliver more than 15 frames per second. **Brightness** is the average luma of the picture from 0 to 100%; near zero usually means a closed privacy shutter or a covered lens. | Resolution | Common name | Aspect | MP | |---|---|---|---| | 640 × 480 | VGA | 4:3 | 0.31 | | 1280 × 720 | HD, 720p | 16:9 | 0.92 | | 1920 × 1080 | Full HD, 1080p | 16:9 | 2.07 | | 2560 × 1440 | QHD, 1440p | 16:9 | 3.69 | | 3840 × 2160 | 4K UHD | 16:9 | 8.29 | {: .data .wct-gt} ## Which resolutions your camera supports The resolution and frame rate menus send *ideal* constraints, as defined in the W3C Media Capture and Streams specification, so the browser picks the closest mode the camera offers instead of failing. **Test resolutions** is stricter: it asks for each size in the table exactly. A size the camera delivers itself counts as supported; one that Chrome can only reach by cropping and scaling a larger mode is reported as scaled, because it holds no more detail than that larger picture shrunk. Lower results than you expect usually mean the camera tops out lower, a USB 2.0 link lacks the bandwidth for high resolutions at high frame rates, or another app has the camera open in a different mode. **Camera capabilities** lists what the browser says the camera can adjust, such as focus or exposure ranges; many webcams report only size and frame rate. ## Mirroring, snapshots and clips The preview is mirrored by default, like a bathroom mirror. Most call apps mirror only your self-view and send the unmirrored picture to others. Snapshots follow the **Mirror** setting; recorded clips are never mirrored, so they show what other people see. ## If the camera will not start - **Permission denied:** click the icon at the left of the address bar, allow the camera and press Start again. - **Blocked by the system:** on Windows open Settings, Privacy & security, Camera; on macOS open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Camera. - **In use:** on Windows a camera usually works in only one app at a time. Close video call apps, OBS and other tabs. - **Not secure:** cameras only work on https pages and on localhost. faq: - q: Is my video uploaded anywhere? a: No. The camera feed goes straight from your device to the video element on this page. Test clips and snapshots are made in your browser and stay in the page's memory until you download them or close the tab. Nothing is sent anywhere. - q: Why is my webcam picture mirrored? a: The preview is flipped horizontally so it behaves like a mirror, which feels natural when you look at yourself. Untick Mirror to see the picture the way other people see it in a call. - q: Why is the measured frame rate lower than my camera's rating? a: Webcams lower their frame rate in dim light to get a brighter exposure. Add light in front of you, close other apps using the camera, and connect it directly to the computer rather than through a hub. - q: It says my camera is in use. What do I do? a: Another program has the camera open. Quit video call apps, streaming software and other browser tabs that use the camera, then press Start again. If nothing else seems to be using it, restarting the browser or unplugging and reconnecting the camera can release it. - q: How do I test the back camera on my phone? a: After you allow access, open the Camera list and choose the rear camera; phones usually list it as back or environment facing. Press Start again if it does not switch automatically. ---
