--- title: "Webcam Test: Check Your Camera Online" description: Test your webcam online with a live preview, real resolution, frame rate, aspect ratio and measured FPS. Switch cameras and save PNG snapshots. category: Device Tests keywords: [webcam test, camera test, test my webcam, webcam resolution test, webcam fps test, online camera test, webcam mirror] howto: | ## How to test your webcam 1. Pick a camera and, if you like, a resolution and frame rate, then press **Start**. Allow camera access when the browser asks. 2. The preview appears with the delivered resolution and frame rate in the corner. The figures below it update every half second. 3. Press **Take snapshot** to capture a full-resolution still, then **Download PNG** to save it. 4. Press **Stop** to release the camera. The webcam light should go out a moment later; if it stays on, another app or tab is still using it. Camera names only appear once you have granted access, because browsers hide device labels from pages without permission. ## What the numbers mean **Resolution**, **Frame rate** and **Aspect ratio** come from `MediaStreamTrack.getSettings()`, which reports the mode the camera is actually running in, not the maximum printed on the box. **Measured FPS** counts the frames the page really displays, using `requestVideoFrameCallback` where the browser supports it. If it is well below the reported rate, the camera is dropping frames. The usual cause is low light: auto-exposure lengthens each exposure, and a 1/15 s exposure cannot deliver more than 15 frames per second. **Brightness** is the average luma of the picture from 0 to 100%. A near-zero value with the camera running usually means a closed privacy shutter or a covered lens. | Resolution | Common name | Aspect | MP | |---|---|---|---| | 640 × 480 | VGA | 4:3 | 0.31 | | 1280 × 720 | HD, 720p | 16:9 | 0.92 | | 1920 × 1080 | Full HD, 1080p | 16:9 | 2.07 | | 2560 × 1440 | QHD, 1440p | 16:9 | 3.69 | | 3840 × 2160 | 4K UHD | 16:9 | 8.29 | {: .data .wct-gt} ## Why you may not get the resolution you picked The resolution and frame rate menus send *ideal* constraints, as defined in the W3C Media Capture and Streams specification. The browser then chooses the closest mode the camera supports instead of failing, and the page shows what you really got. Common reasons for a lower result: the camera tops out lower, a USB 2.0 connection lacks the bandwidth for high resolutions at high frame rates, or another app already has the camera open in a different mode. Chrome can also crop and scale frames to reach a requested size; the **Resize mode** row shows `crop-and-scale` when that happened. The **Maximum resolution** row lists the largest size the camera reports it can deliver, where the browser exposes it. ## Mirroring and snapshots The preview is mirrored by default, so moving your right hand moves the image on the right, as in a bathroom mirror. Most call apps mirror only your self-view and send the unmirrored picture to others. Snapshots follow the preview: with **Mirror** on they are saved mirrored, with it off they match what other people see. Snapshots are saved at the camera's full delivered resolution as lossless PNG files. ## If the camera will not start - **Permission denied:** click the icon at the left of the address bar, allow the camera and press Start again. - **Blocked by the system:** on Windows open Settings, Privacy & security, Camera; on macOS open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Camera. - **In use:** on Windows a camera usually works in only one app at a time. Close video call apps, OBS and other tabs. - **Not secure:** cameras only work on https pages and on localhost. faq: - q: Is my video recorded or uploaded? a: No. The camera feed goes straight from your device to the video element on this page. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere, and snapshots stay in your browser until you choose Download PNG. - q: Why is my webcam picture mirrored? a: The preview is flipped horizontally so it behaves like a mirror, which feels natural when you look at yourself. Untick Mirror to see the picture the way other people see it in a call. - q: Why is the measured frame rate lower than my camera's rating? a: Webcams lower their frame rate in dim light to get a brighter exposure. Add light in front of you, close other apps using the camera, and connect it directly to the computer rather than through a hub. - q: It says my camera is in use. What do I do? a: Another program has the camera open. Quit video call apps, streaming software and other browser tabs that use the camera, then press Start again. If nothing else seems to be using it, restarting the browser or unplugging and reconnecting the camera can release it. - q: How do I test the back camera on my phone? a: After you allow access, open the Camera list and choose the rear camera; phones usually list it as back or environment facing. Press Start again if it does not switch automatically. ---
