Every page appears as a thumbnail. Pick one of the four ways to split, check the summary under the settings, and press the button below it. One new PDF is saved straight away; several are saved together in a ZIP file, and each one is also listed with its own download button. | Way to split | What you get | Example on a 10-page PDF | |---|---|---| | Custom ranges | One PDF per range | `1-3, 4-7, 8-end`: 3 PDFs | | Every N pages | A PDF every N pages | 2: five PDFs of 2 pages | | Every page | One PDF per page | 10 PDFs of 1 page | | Select pages | The pages you pick | pages 2 and 5: 1 PDF | Each thumbnail is labelled with the PDF it goes into, and pages that go into none are marked *Left out*. ## Writing page ranges Separate ranges with commas: `1-3, 7, 9-12`. A dash joins the first and last page of a range, `end` stands for the last page, `8-` runs to the end and `-3` starts at page 1. A range written backwards, `5-1`, saves the pages in reverse order. Write page numbers without thousands separators. Numbers count pages from the start of the file, so the first page is 1 even when it is printed as *i* or *cover*. Tick **Merge all ranges into one PDF** to pull several parts out of a document into a single file, in the order you typed them. A page can be in more than one range. ## Extracting or deleting pages In **Select pages** mode, click a thumbnail to pick it, or drag across several. On a touch screen, tap a page, or touch and hold one and then drag. Shift+click picks every page between the last click and this one, and the buttons pick all, none, odd or even pages, or invert the choice. You can also type the page numbers. The pages are saved in their original order, as one PDF or as one PDF each. To delete pages from a PDF, pick the pages to remove and press **Invert**, then **Extract**: the other pages are saved as one PDF. The [PDF merger](/merge-pdf/) can also remove, rotate and reorder pages one by one. ## Does splitting reduce quality? No. Pages are copied as they are, not redrawn or compressed again, so text stays sharp and searchable and pictures keep every pixel. Each file carries the fonts and images its own pages use, which is why the parts of a split can add up to more than the original. Web links are kept, and so are links between two pages that end up in the same file. Bookmarks, digital signatures, attachments and document properties such as the title and author belong to the whole document and are not carried over, and form fields keep their look but may no longer be fillable. ## Password-protected PDFs A PDF that opens without a password but blocks copying or changes is split directly. For a PDF that needs a password to open, type that password when asked. The new files have no password; to combine pages from several PDFs instead, use the [PDF merger](/merge-pdf/). Scans and photos that are not PDFs yet can be turned into one with the [image to PDF converter](/image-to-pdf/), and single pages can be saved as pictures with the [PDF to JPG converter](/pdf-to-jpg/). [Image to text](/image-to-text/) copies the words out of a scanned PDF. faq: - q: Are my PDFs uploaded to a server? a: No. The PDF is read, previewed and split by JavaScript in this page, on your own device. Nothing is sent over the network, so you can split confidential documents safely. - q: How do I split a PDF in half? a: Use **Custom ranges**, which suggests two halves when you open a file, such as `1-25, 26-50` for 50 pages. You can also choose **Every N pages** and enter half the page count. - q: Is there a size or page limit? a: The PDF can be up to 1 GB, and one split makes at most 10,000 files with up to 20,000 pages in all. Everything is held in your device's memory, so very large files split more reliably on a computer than on a phone. - q: Can I split a scanned PDF? a: Yes. A scanned page is a picture inside the PDF, and it is copied into the new file unchanged. Splitting does not add a text layer, so a scan that could not be searched before still cannot be searched. ---
