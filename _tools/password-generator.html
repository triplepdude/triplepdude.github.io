Nothing is sent or stored. category: Security & Network keywords: [password generator, random password generator, strong password generator, password generator 16 characters, password generator 12 characters, wifi password generator, pin generator, secure password generator] howto: | ## How to generate a strong password A random 16-character password appears when the page opens. Press **New password** for another one, or **Copy** to put it on the clipboard; while you are working in the tool, the keys N and C do the same. Changing an option draws a fresh password straight away. - **Length**: drag the slider, type a number from 4 to 128 or pick a preset. Length adds more strength than any other setting. - **Characters**: every kind you tick appears at least once. If a site accepts only some symbols, choose **Common** (the eight symbols on the number keys 1 to 8 of a US keyboard) or type the allowed ones under **Your own list**; letters, digits and spaces typed there are ignored. - **Avoid look-alikes** leaves out 0, O, o, 1, l, I and the vertical bar, for passwords you read out or type from paper. - **Exclude characters** removes any character a site refuses. - **How many** makes up to 100 at once, to copy together or save as a .txt file. The options are kept in the page address, so a bookmark brings them back. The passwords never are. ## How long should a password be? | Length | Entropy (all four kinds) | Average time at 100 billion guesses a second | |---|---|---| | 8 | 51.3 bits | about 3.9 hours | | 12 | 78.1 bits | about 53 thousand years | | 16 | 104.6 bits | about 4.8 trillion years | | 20 | 130.9 bits | about 4.1 × 10²⁰ years | Twelve random characters hold out against one fast attacker, but at 100 trillion guesses a second, a large cluster of graphics cards, the average falls to 53 years. The US agency CISA recommends at least 16 characters. NIST's rules for websites (SP 800-63B-4, July 2025) require at least 15 characters for a password used on its own and 8 when a second factor is also asked for. Chrome suggests 15 letters and digits, and Apple's generated passwords have 20 characters, mostly lower case with two hyphens, for 71 bits. ## How the strength is worked out Each character comes from `crypto.getRandomValues`, the browser's secure random source, through rejection sampling, so no character is more likely than another. When a password misses a kind you ticked, the whole password is drawn again rather than a character being forced into place, which keeps every allowed password equally likely. The entropy is log2 of the exact number of passwords your settings allow, and assumes the attacker knows those settings. The scale and the guessing speeds are the same as in the [passphrase generator](/passphrase-generator/). The [password strength checker](/password-strength-checker/) uses zxcvbn's speeds (10 billion a second for a fast hash) and looks for the patterns people choose, so its times for the same password are different. ## Wi-Fi passwords and PINs A WPA2 Wi-Fi password must have 8 to 63 printable ASCII characters. The **Wi-Fi** preset makes 20 letters and digits without look-alikes (116.1 bits), which are easy to type on a TV or a phone, and a [WiFi QR code](/wifi-qr-code-generator/) saves typing it at all. A 6-digit PIN has only 19.9 bits: it is safe because a phone or a bank card locks after a few wrong tries. ## What this page does not do It cannot save or sync passwords, or check them against lists of leaked ones. Keep them in a password manager, which also fills them in for you. Copied text can stay in clipboard history (Windows+V) or be synced to your other devices, so clear it after pasting. faq: - q: Is it safe to use an online password generator? a: This one makes passwords in your browser with its built-in secure random generator. Nothing is sent to a server, logged or stored, and it keeps working if you go offline after the page loads. Only the options, never a password, go into the page address. - q: Why does a website reject my password? a: Each site sets its own rules, such as a maximum length, a kind of character it insists on, or the few symbols it accepts. Match them with the length, the character boxes, Your own list and Exclude characters. NIST advises websites to accept at least 64 characters and tells them not to demand mixes of character types, but many still do. - q: Is a passphrase better than a random password? a: At the same entropy both are equally hard to guess. A random password packs the strength into fewer characters, while random words are easier to remember and type; CISA suggests 4 to 7 unrelated words. The UK's NCSC promotes three random words, but says passwords made by a password manager are stronger. - q: How do I remember a random password? a: Let a password manager remember them and fill them in, and learn only the few you type yourself, such as the manager's main password and your computer login. A passphrase is easier to learn for those. Never reuse a password on two sites. ---
