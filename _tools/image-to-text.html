Press **Choose images or PDF**, drop files on the box, paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V (⌘V on a Mac) or press **Take photo**. JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF, GIF, BMP, TIFF, SVG and PDF files work, up to 50 at a time. 2. Pick the **Language of the text**. Before the first picture is read, the page downloads the OCR engine and the language data from this site: about 4.5 MB for English, and 0.7 to 3 MB for each other language. 3. The reading is done by Tesseract, the open-source OCR engine first written at Hewlett-Packard and developed by Google from 2006 to 2017, here compiled to run inside your browser. ## Getting better results | Problem | What helps | |---|---| | Small text, as in most screenshots | **Enlarge small text** on Automatic makes the picture 2 to 4 times bigger when its lines are under 22 pixels tall. Tesseract's documentation says that below a lower-case *x* of 10 pixels there is very little chance of an accurate result. | | Shadows, or a grey or yellowed page | **Greyscale and even out lighting** brightens each part of the picture to match the paper around it. | | Light text on a dark background | With the same option ticked, a mostly dark picture is inverted first: Tesseract 4 and later reads dark text on a light background best. | | A tilted photo | Tilts of a few degrees are straightened. Use **Rotate page** for a picture that is sideways or upside down. | | Wrong accents or letters | Choose the language of the text. Read with English data, the Spanish *mañana* comes out as *mafiana*. | | Columns, signs or a single line | Try **Layout**: *Scattered text* looks for words all over the picture, *One line* reads the picture as a single line. | | Blurred letters | Take the photo again in even light, closer, with the camera parallel to the page. | **Black and white** turns every pixel black or white at the level that best separates text from paper (Otsu's method), which can help with faint print. Tick **Show it as it is read** to see the picture exactly as the OCR engine gets it, after these changes. ## Confidence for each line Every line found is outlined on the picture and listed with its number and Tesseract's confidence, from 0 to 100%. Lines under 80% are marked **Check** and have a dashed outline: they are the likeliest to hold mistakes. A line at 95% can still have one wrong digit, so check amounts, dates and codes before you rely on them. ## Scanned PDFs Each page of a PDF is read on its own, up to 100 pages per file. With **Use the text stored in PDFs** ticked, a page that already holds text, such as a PDF saved from Word, has its text copied from the file; scanned pages are drawn at up to 300 dpi and read as pictures. A PDF that is locked with a password asks for it, and the password stays on your device. To read a few pages of a long PDF, take them out first with [Split PDF](/split-pdf/).
