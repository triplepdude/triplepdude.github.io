For timed work sessions with breaks, use the [focus timer](/pomodoro-timer/). ## Lap time and split time On Casio and most sports stopwatches, a **split** is the time from the start to a point in the race, and a **lap** is the time of one segment. The table shows both: **Lap time** is the time since the previous lap, and **Total** is the split. Apps use the words loosely: Strava, for example, calls the time of each mile or kilometre a split. Say you press Lap at 1:05.123, 2:06.123 and 3:08.623: | Lap | Lap time | Total | |---|---|---| | 1 | 01:05.123 | 01:05.123 | | 2 | 01:01.000 | 02:06.123 | | 3 | 01:02.500 | 03:08.623 | Lap 2 is marked fastest and lap 1 slowest, and the average lap is 3:08.623 ÷ 3 = 1:02.874. When you stop, the lap in progress becomes the last row of the copy and the download, but only laps recorded with Lap are compared. **Copy laps** gives a table that pastes into a spreadsheet, with a decimal comma if your browser's language writes one (1,5); the CSV file always has full stops. ## How accurate is it? The time is read from your device's clock, which browsers give to a page to a millisecond or better, and it is worked out from time stamps, so a busy or slow page cannot make it fall behind. The digits are redrawn with the screen, usually 60 times a second, so the last digit jumps in steps of about 16 ms while it runs; the time shown when you stop, and every lap, is exact to the millisecond. Your reaction time is the real limit. In a study of 26 timekeepers, trained for the study, timing 200 m sprints with handheld stopwatches, each time was off by 0.15 seconds on average compared with electronic timing, and too fast in two cases out of three (Hetzler et al., 2008). The [reaction time test](/reaction-time-test/) shows your own delay. ## In the background, asleep or after a reload The stopwatch keeps counting in a background tab, in a minimised window and with the screen off. On most systems the browser's precise clock pauses while the computer or phone sleeps, so the stopwatch also compares the time of day and adds any gap of more than a second; setting the clock back is ignored. The start time and the laps are kept in the page address after the # sign, so a reload, or reopening the tab from your history, carries on where you were.
