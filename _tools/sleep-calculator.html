Runs in your browser. category: Calculators keywords: [sleep calculator, bedtime calculator, sleep cycle calculator, what time should i go to bed, wake up time calculator, calculate when to sleep, how much sleep do i need, what time should i wake up] howto: | ## How to use the sleep calculator Choose whether you want to wake up at a set time, go to bed at a set time or go to sleep now, and type the time: *6:30*, *630a*, *18:30* and *noon* all work. On a 12-hour clock a bedtime without AM or PM, such as *11*, is read as night, and the line under the box shows how the time was read. The times update as you type and are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. Each option counts back (or forward) whole sleep cycles plus the minutes you take to fall asleep. To wake up at 6:30 AM with 90-minute cycles and 15 minutes to fall asleep: | Cycles | Sleep | In bed | Asleep | |---|---|---|---| | 6 | 9 hours | 9:15 PM | 9:30 PM | | 5 | 7 hours 30 minutes | 10:45 PM | 11:00 PM | | 4 | 6 hours | 12:15 AM | 12:30 AM | | 3 | 4 hours 30 minutes | 1:45 AM | 2:00 AM | The times are for the coming night on your device's clock, so on the night the clocks change they still give the full sleep shown. To count how long you actually slept, use the [hours calculator](/hours-calculator/). ## How much sleep do I need? Total sleep matters more than where a cycle ends. These are the CDC's hours a day (15 May 2024, from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the National Sleep Foundation); the NHS gives 7 to 9 for adults. | Age | Hours | |---|---| | 0 to 3 months | 14 to 17 | | 4 to 12 months | 12 to 16, naps included | | 1 to 2 years | 11 to 14, naps included | | 3 to 5 years | 10 to 13, naps included | | 6 to 12 years | 9 to 12 | | 13 to 17 years | 8 to 10 | | 18 to 60 years | 7 or more | | 61 to 64 years | 7 to 9 | | 65 and older | 7 to 8 | Options with less sleep than your age group's minimum are marked. The box above them gives the bedtime or wake-up window from the hours alone, which is how the AASM's own bedtime calculator works. Under 6 the page plans by hours only, because naps count towards the total and a baby's cycle is about 50 minutes at 3 months. ## Is a sleep cycle always 90 minutes? No. The US National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute puts a cycle at 80 to 100 minutes, four to six a night. The first cycle is usually 70 to 100 minutes and later ones 90 to 120 (Institute of Medicine, 2006). Across 6,064 cycles recorded in a sleep laboratory the median was 96 minutes, with large differences between people (Cajochen and others, 2024). Treat the times as a planning aid, and change the cycle length if you know your own. ## Why do I wake up groggy? The fog after waking, called sleep inertia, is worse when an alarm wakes you from deep (slow-wave) sleep, and it rarely lasts more than 30 minutes unless you are very short of sleep (Tassi and Muzet, 2000). Most deep sleep comes in the first third of the night, so a morning alarm is less likely to catch you in it. ## Setting the alarm A web page cannot tell which stage of sleep you are in, wake you in light sleep or reliably ring on a locked phone, so set the time in your phone's alarm: on Android in the Clock app's Bedtime tab, on an iPhone as a sleep schedule in the Health app (its wake-up alarm can be changed in Clock). For a short nap, the [online timer](/online-timer/) rings in an open tab. The NHS advises seeing a GP if changing your sleep habits has not helped, you have had trouble sleeping for months, you always feel very tired during the day, or your breathing stops and starts while you sleep. faq: - q: Is 7.5 hours of sleep better than 8? a: Not for any reason a calculator can know. Cycles vary from about 80 to 120 minutes, so 7.5 hours is not reliably five whole cycles, and 8 hours gives you half an hour more sleep. If you wake up rested after 8 hours, keep the 8. - q: How long should it take to fall asleep? a: Up to about 30 minutes. A National Sleep Foundation panel (Ohayon and others, 2017) rated 15 minutes or less, and also 16 to 30 minutes, as signs of good sleep quality. Taking more than an hour was rated as poor sleep quality at every age. Set the minutes to fall asleep to what is usual for you. - q: Are sleep calculators accurate? a: The arithmetic is exact, but the cycle length behind it is an average. Your own cycles may be shorter or longer, and they change through the night, so a time chosen to match the end of a cycle can still fall in the middle of one. Getting the hours your age needs is the part that is backed by evidence. - q: Can this page wake me up? a: No. It has no alarm, because a web page cannot ring reliably on a locked or sleeping phone. Copy the time you choose into your phone's Clock app, or into the Health app's sleep schedule on an iPhone. - q: Is anything I type stored or sent? a: "No. The times are worked out in your browser and nothing is uploaded, saved or put in a cookie. Once you change something, the address keeps your settings after the # sign, as in #wake=06:30, so you can bookmark or share them. Browsers never send that part of the address to the website." ---
