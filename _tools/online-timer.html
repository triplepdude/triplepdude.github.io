--- title: "Online Timer: Countdown Timer with Alarm" description: Set a countdown with presets or any hours, minutes and seconds, pick an alarm sound, run several timers at once and go full screen. Runs in your browser. category: Date & Time keywords: [online timer, timer, countdown timer, timer with alarm, 10 minute timer, 5 minute timer, multiple timers, full screen timer, timer with sound, kitchen timer, egg timer, cooking timer, countdown clock] howto: | ## How to set a timer Type the hours, minutes and seconds and press **Start** or Enter, or press one of the presets from 1 minute to 1 hour, which start at once. Decimals work in every box: 1.5 or 1,5 hours is 1 hour 30 minutes, and 90 minutes becomes 1:30:00. A number such as 1,500 could be 1.5 or 1500, so the box asks you to type one of those instead. **Pause** stops the countdown and **Resume** carries on; **+1 min** adds a minute, even to a timer that has just rung; **Reset** goes back to the time you set. While the alarm rings, the main button reads **Stop alarm**. To ring at a time of day instead, use the [alarm clock](/alarm-clock/); for work sessions with breaks, the [focus timer](/pomodoro-timer/). For a 10-minute timer you can also open `/online-timer/#10m` and bookmark it. The address takes hours, minutes and seconds such as `#1h30m` or `#90s`, clock times such as `#5:00` or `#1:30:00`, a plain number of minutes (`#10`), and several timers separated by commas (`#10m,3m`). | Key | What it does | |---|---| | Space | Start, pause or resume; stops a ringing alarm | | R | Reset a paused or finished timer | | F | Full screen on or off | | Esc | Stop the alarm; leave full screen | Keys act on the timer you used last, and not while you type in a box. ## Several timers at once **Add a timer** for each thing you are timing, up to 10, and give each a name such as "Pasta". Each one counts down and rings on its own, and the tab title shows the time left on the one that ends next, so you can follow it from another tab. Names stay out of the title, which others can see when you share your screen. ## Is it accurate in a background tab? The countdown runs to a fixed end time on your device's clock, shown under the digits, rather than counting ticks, so it never runs slow. Chrome runs a background tab's timers at most once a second, and once a page has been hidden for 5 minutes without playing sound, repeating timers only once a minute. So the check for the end is started straight from your click, and once a click has let the page play sound, the alarm is also scheduled in advance on the sound card's clock, which keeps time in a background tab. The countdown in the tab title is redrawn by a repeating timer, so after those 5 minutes it can lag by up to a minute; the alarm does not. If the computer sleeps, the timer catches up when it wakes. A timer that ended more than a minute earlier shows the time it ended instead of ringing late. Phones may pause pages they do not show. On an iPhone or iPad, Safari pauses the page soon after you switch apps or lock the screen, so the alarm cannot sound until you come back to it, and not at all once the timer ended more than a minute before. The page asks to keep the screen on while a timer runs; for an alarm you cannot miss, use the phone's Clock app. ## No sound on an iPhone? - Most browsers let a page play sound only after you tap or click on it; Start or a preset counts. After a reload, tap anywhere on the page once. - Safari mutes sound that a page makes itself, like this alarm, while the iPhone is in silent mode, though music and videos still play. - Press **Test sound** to check the volume, or try the [speaker test](/speaker-test/). To time something as it happens, use the [stopwatch](/stopwatch/). faq: - q: Is anything uploaded or saved? a: "No. The timers run in your browser. Their lengths, names and end times are kept only in the page address after the # sign, which browsers never send to a server, so a reload keeps them; nothing goes into cookies or browser storage." - q: Can I get a notification when a timer ends? a: Yes, in desktop browsers. Tick "Show a notification" and allow notifications when the browser asks. Chrome on Android can show them only through a service worker, and Safari on iPhone and iPad only for web apps added to the Home Screen; this page uses neither, so there the alarm and the tab title tell you instead. - q: What happens if I close the tab? a: A closed page cannot ring. Reopen it from your history, or with Ctrl+Shift+T (Cmd+Shift+T on a Mac), and the timers come back from the page address; one that is still running carries on, and one that ended meanwhile shows when it ended. - q: How do I make the alarm louder? a: The volume slider works within your device's own volume, so turn that up too and check with Test sound. The Beeps and Alarm clock sounds are pitched around 2,000 Hz, close to the 2,000 to 5,000 Hz range where hearing is most sensitive, so at the same volume they sound louder than Soft. ---
