Pick a level or a size limit; nothing is uploaded. category: Image & PDF keywords: [compress pdf, pdf compress, pdf compressor, reduce pdf size, pdf size reducer, reduce pdf file size, how to compress a pdf, compress pdf to 1mb, compress pdf to 100kb] howto: | ## How to compress a PDF 1. Press **Choose PDF files**, or drop one or more PDFs on the page. 2. Pick a level. **Recommended** suits email and uploads; **Fit a size** finds the sharpest copy under a limit such as 1 MB, or Gmail's 25 MB. 3. The files are compressed one after another. Press **Preview** to compare a page of the copy with the original at actual size, then **Download**, or **Download all** for a ZIP file. Nothing is uploaded: this page reads and rewrites the files on your device. ## What makes a PDF big, and what changes Almost always pictures: scanned pages, photos and screenshots stored sharper than the page needs. A 12-megapixel phone photo placed 10 cm wide holds about 1,000 pixels per inch, nearly seven times the 150 that Recommended keeps. Each picture is measured at the size it is drawn, reduced to the level's resolution and saved as a JPEG. Photos stored without loss become JPEGs too, while charts, drawings and screenshots stay lossless and black-and-white scans are left alone. A JPEG already at or below the level is not touched, so nothing is compressed twice. Text, fonts, links, bookmarks, form fields, tags for screen readers and attachments stay as they are. The rest is stored compactly: a picture or font saved twice is kept once, and page thumbnails, unused objects and the editing data some apps leave behind are removed. | Level | Pictures | 300 dpi scans | Photo pages | Text and forms | |---|---|---|---|---| | Light | 220 ppi, JPEG quality 85 | 39–42% smaller | 69–83% | 4–28% | | Recommended | 150 ppi, quality 75 | 73–75% | 90–94% | 4–28% | | Strong | 100 ppi, quality 60 | 88–89% | 97–98% | 4–28% | Measured on eight-page colour and grey scans, two six-page photo brochures, IRS Publication 17 and Form 1040. ## Getting under 1 MB, or any limit **Fit a size** searches seven levels, from the sharpest, 220 ppi, down to 72 ppi at quality 50, and keeps the sharpest copy that fits. 1 MB counts as 1,000,000 bytes, so the copy also passes a limit of 1,048,576. If even the smallest copy is too big, what is left is mostly text, fonts or pictures this page leaves alone: split the file with [Split PDF](/split-pdf/) and send it in parts. ## Why a PDF may not get smaller - It is mostly text, like reports and forms: expect 4–28%. - It is a black-and-white scan, stored with CCITT or JBIG2 compression, which is already about as small as it gets. - Its pictures are already at or below the level: try **Strong**. - It is a PDF/X file for print, whose CMYK pictures are kept as they are. A copy is offered only when it is at least 1% smaller, or when **Fit a size** gets it under the limit you chose. ## Before you send the copy - CMYK pictures become RGB, which suits screens, except in PDF/X files: keep the original for a print shop. - A digital signature no longer validates, because the bytes change. - A password-protected PDF needs its password, and the copy keeps the same passwords and restrictions. To remove a password, use [Unlock PDF](/unlock-pdf/). - **Strong** makes small print in scans soft: check it in the preview. It needs a current browser: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari 18 or later. faq: - q: Is my PDF uploaded to a server? a: "No. The page reads, compresses and saves your files with JavaScript on your own device, and nothing is kept after you close the tab. The copy keeps the same passwords, encryption and restrictions. A file whose author forbids changes also needs its permissions password, the second password set when it was protected. - q: How big can an email attachment be? a: Gmail takes up to 25 MB of attachments per message on a personal account and turns bigger files into Google Drive links.
