The standard passage reworks sections 1.10.32 and 1.10.33, and *lorem* is the tail of *dolorem*. Cicero wrote "Neque porro quisquam est, qui dolorem ipsum quia dolor sit amet, consectetur, adipisci velit", which H. Rackham's 1914 translation gives as "Nor again is there anyone who loves or pursues or desires to obtain pain of itself, because it is pain". Richard McClintock, a Latin scholar at Hampden–Sydney College, traced the text by looking up its rare word *consectetur* and described the source in a 1994 letter to *Before & After* magazine. That letter also said printers had used the text since the 1500s, a claim many sites still repeat; McClintock has since said the date was a guess with no evidence behind it. The earliest known uses are on Letraset dry-transfer lettering sheets in the 1960s, and the desktop publishing program Aldus PageMaker included it in 1985. ## Is lorem ipsum real Latin? Only in part. The words come from Cicero, but they were cut, reordered, changed and added to, so the text means nothing, and *lorem* itself is not a Latin word.
