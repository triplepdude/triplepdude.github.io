Choose what to save, press **Cut**, then **Download**. ## Cutting without losing quality An MP3 is a chain of frames of 1,152 samples, 26 ms at 44.1 kHz, and most frames borrow bytes from the frames before them. **Exact copy** copies the frames you selected as they are, with the two or three before your start that the first ones need, and writes a new LAME tag that tells the player how many samples to skip at each end. Nothing is decoded and encoded again, so the sound is the original's, bit for bit, from your start to your end. Players that read the LAME tag, such as Chrome and ffmpeg, start and stop on the exact sample. Players that ignore it also play the frames before your start: at 44.1 kHz, up to about a tenth of a second. Fades, a change of volume, mono and cutting out a part all change the sound, so they are re-encoded, at the bitrate you choose (the file's own to begin with). Re-encoded MP3s and WAVs keep the exact length too. ## Cutting out a part, or splitting | What to save | You get | |---|---| | The selection | the part between the handles | | Everything but the selection | the file without that part, the join crossfaded over 20 ms | | The selection split at the markers | one file a part, and a ZIP of them all | To split a long recording, such as a concert or an audiobook, play it and press **M** wherever a new part begins, or use **Split every** with *5:00* for parts of five minutes. Each part keeps the file's tags and cover, with "(part 2)" and so on after the title. ## Making a ringtone **Make it a ringtone** keeps 29.5 seconds from the start handle, adds a 1-second fade-out and picks MP3.
