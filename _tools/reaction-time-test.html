Runs in your browser. category: Device Tests keywords: [reaction time test, reaction test, reflex test, reaction speed test, average reaction time, reaction time by age, audio reaction time test, click reaction test] howto: | ## How to take the reaction time test Click the box, tap it or press Space to start a round. The box turns red; when it turns green, click, tap or press Space as fast as you can. The wait is a random 2 to 5 seconds, so it cannot be timed. After 5 rounds you get your average, median and best time, and how your average compares with other people's. - **Too soon** means you pressed before the green, or less than 100 ms after it. Reaction time research counts a response that fast as a guess, so the round is repeated and counted as a false start. - **Beep** replaces the green with a short beep and leaves the box unchanged, to test your hearing instead of your sight, or for use with a screen reader. Use **Play the beep** to check the volume first; on an iPhone or iPad, silent mode mutes it. Bluetooth headphones add a delay of their own, so use the built-in speaker or wired headphones. - The clock starts at the screen frame that turns the box green, or when the browser expects the beep to reach the speaker, and stops at the time stamp of your press, so your screen's and mouse's delay is included. ## What is a good reaction time? Human Benchmark, the best-known online test, gives a median of 273 ms and a mean of 284 ms for the 81 million clicks it has recorded. In a lab study of 1,469 adults using a gaming mouse the average was 231 ms, of which 17.8 ms was the delay of the screen and mouse (Woods et al., 2015). The percentile shown with your result compares your average with the 70,940 scores in the results chart on Human Benchmark's test page. That chart's median is just under 230 ms, well below the 273 ms of all recorded clicks, so it is a demanding comparison: | Your average | Faster than | |---|---| | 150 ms | 99% | | 175 ms | 92% | | 200 ms | 74% | | 225 ms | 53% | | 250 ms | 33% | | 275 ms | 19% | | 300 ms | 11% | | 350 ms | 5% | ## Reaction time by age | Age | Average in the lab study | |---|---| | 18 to 24 | 218 ms | | 32 to 38 | 225 ms | | 46 to 51 | 234 ms | | 59 to 65 | 239 ms | That is about half a millisecond slower for each year of age. A survey of 7,130 adults in the UK found that simple reaction time changes little until about 50 and slows after that. Expect higher numbers on an ordinary computer or phone than in the lab. ## Laptops, phones and 144 Hz monitors A screen can only show the green on a new frame: every 16.7 ms at 60 Hz and every 6.9 ms at 144 Hz, and a change takes at least one frame to reach the screen. A 144 Hz monitor therefore takes about 10 ms or more off your times without changing your reflexes. Below the box the page shows how many frames a second your browser is drawing; the [screen resolution checker](/screen-resolution/) shows your refresh rate. Many laptop screens run at 60 Hz, and Human Benchmark puts the delay of a typical desktop or laptop at about 30 ms. Phones add more: in a 2023 study of 26 smartphones, touch and display delay together came to between 35 and 140 ms. Sound is quicker than sight. Textbook averages for young adults are about 160 ms for a sound and 190 ms for a light, so with wired sound the beep usually gives lower times. To measure how fast you click rather than react, try the [CPS test](/cps-test/). faq: - q: Is my reaction time saved or sent anywhere? a: No. The test runs in your browser with JavaScript and keeps your times only in memory, so they are gone when you reload or close the page. Nothing is uploaded, stored or tracked. - q: Why does it say Too soon when I clicked after it turned green? a: Any press less than 100 ms after the green counts as a false start. Reaction time research, such as the psychomotor vigilance test used in sleep studies, treats responses that fast as guesses made before the signal was seen. Wait for the change and the next press will count. - q: How can I improve my reaction time? a: Get enough sleep. Sleep loss is one of the best-documented causes of slow and missed reactions, which is why sleep research measures it with a reaction time test. People who play action video games regularly tend to react faster in studies, although whether gaming itself speeds you up is disputed. For a better score, test on a computer monitor rather than a TV, which Human Benchmark says can add as much as 150 ms, and with a mouse or keyboard rather than a touch screen. ---
