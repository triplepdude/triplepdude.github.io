Download the JPGs one by one or zipped together. ## Why you have an AVIF AVIF is an image format from the Alliance for Open Media: pictures compressed with the AV1 video codec, inside the same HEIF container that iPhone HEIC photos use. It usually makes smaller files than JPG at the same quality, so image services send it to every browser that asks for it. Chrome, Edge and Firefox put image/avif first in the list of formats they request, which means a picture saved from such a site often arrives as .avif, on a computer and in Chrome on an Android phone alike. Phone cameras themselves save JPG or HEIC, so an AVIF on your phone most likely came from the web. A picture that arrived as .webp instead converts with [WebP to JPG](/webp-to-jpg/). ## Programs that still refuse it Browsers caught up slowly: Chrome opened AVIF from version 85, Firefox from 93, Safari fully from 16.4 and Edge only from 121, in January 2024. Photoshop opened and saved it natively only from version 26.8, released in June 2025, and many older photo viewers and editors cannot open it at all. Upload forms that list only JPG and PNG turn it away too. A JPG avoids all of that. ## How it is decoded No extra code is downloaded: the AVIF is decoded by your browser's own AVIF support, then saved as JPG. In a browser without that support, the file list says so. It also names a file that stopped downloading part-way or is damaged, which Chrome would otherwise turn into a blank picture. An animated AVIF, which is rare, gives its first frame. ## Colour and HDR - **Wide colour.** An AVIF tagged as Display P3 or BT.2020 has its colours translated into sRGB, the colour space viewers assume for a JPG unless it is tagged otherwise. Shades more vivid than sRGB allows are moved to the nearest ones it has. - **HDR.** An HDR AVIF (PQ or HLG) stores brightness beyond what a normal JPG holds. The browser maps it into standard range, so highlights look less brilliant than on an HDR screen. Keep the AVIF for HDR viewing. - **Bit depth.** AVIF may use 10 or 12 bits per colour, while the JPGs made here have 8. The difference is rarely visible, except perhaps as faint banding in a smooth sky.
