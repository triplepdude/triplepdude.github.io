--- title: "Mic Test: Check Your Microphone Online" description: Test your microphone online with a live level meter and waveform. Check peak dBFS, switch mics, and record a short clip to hear how you sound. category: Device Tests keywords: [mic test, microphone test, test my mic, online mic test, microphone level meter, mic volume test, record and play back mic] howto: | ## How to test your microphone 1. Choose a microphone if you have more than one, then press **Start**. Your browser asks for permission the first time; allow it. 2. Speak at your normal distance. The bar moves, the waveform draws your voice, and a green message confirms that sound is reaching the browser. 3. Press **Record** to capture a 5-second clip (or 10 or 30 seconds). It plays back automatically when it finishes, so you hear what the browser captures with the current filter settings. 4. Press **Stop** when you are done. The page stops every audio track, so the browser's recording indicator switches off. Device names only appear after you grant access, because browsers hide them from pages that have no permission. If the level stays flat, try the other entries in the list: laptops often expose a built-in array, a headset and a "communications" device separately. ## Reading the meter Levels are shown in dBFS, decibels relative to full scale. 0 dBFS is the largest value digital audio can hold, so every reading is zero or negative. **Peak** is the loudest single sample in the last 2,048 samples (about 43 ms at 48 kHz), and **Level (RMS)** is the root-mean-square level over the same window. For a steady sine wave the RMS reading sits 3 dB below the peak; speech is spikier, so the gap is wider. **Peak hold** keeps the loudest peak since you pressed Start, which is the number to check before recording. The analysis runs at the microphone's own sample rate where the browser allows it, because resampling can overshoot and read slightly above 0 dBFS. | Peak while you talk | What it means | |---|---| | Below −50 dBFS | Barely audible: muted, wrong device, or input volume near zero | | −50 to −25 dBFS | Quiet: raise the input volume or move closer | | −25 to −6 dBFS | A good range for calls and voice recording | | Above −6 dBFS | Loud: little headroom, plosives may distort | | Clipping | Samples hit full scale and are flattened; lower the input gain | {: .data .mict-gt} The fill colour follows the same bands: green up to −18 dBFS, amber to −6 dBFS, and red above. ## Echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain By default, browsers run microphone audio through the same kind of processing used for video calls. The three stages are the `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` constraints of the W3C Media Capture and Streams specification. The checkboxes above switch them on or off where the browser supports it, and the settings table shows what was actually applied. Automatic gain gradually raises quiet input and lowers loud input, so turn it off when you want to judge the real volume set in your operating system. Turning all three off gives the rawest signal the browser allows; music apps often do this because the filters are tuned for speech. ## When the microphone will not start - **Permission denied:** click the icon at the left of the address bar, allow the microphone for this site and press Start again. - **Blocked by the system:** on Windows open Settings, Privacy & security, Microphone; on macOS open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone, and allow your browser. - **In use:** another app may hold the device exclusively. Close call apps and other tabs that use the microphone. - **Not secure:** browsers only allow microphones on https pages and on localhost. faq: - q: Is my voice recorded or uploaded anywhere? a: No. The audio is analysed by your browser's Web Audio engine on your own device. A test clip stays in the page's memory until you record a new one, reload or close the tab, and it leaves your computer only if you press Download. - q: The meter moves here, but people cannot hear me in Zoom or Teams. Why? a: Call apps pick their own input device. Open the app's audio settings and choose the same microphone that works here, and check that it is not muted inside the app or by a headset mute button. - q: What is a good microphone level? a: For speech, aim for peaks between about −25 and −6 dBFS at your normal speaking distance. Peaks near 0 dBFS or the clipping warning mean the input is too loud and will distort. - q: Why does my recording sound different from the live meter? a: The clip is compressed, with the Opus codec in current browsers (AAC in older Safari versions), and includes whatever echo cancellation and noise suppression are switched on. Turn the filters off and record again to hear the raw input. - q: Why is the microphone list empty or showing generic names? a: Browsers only reveal device names after you allow microphone access for the page. Press Start once and the list fills in; if it stays empty, the system does not see any input device. ---
