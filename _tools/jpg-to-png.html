Phone photos tagged as rotated are turned upright first. The PNG holds exactly the pixels your browser decodes from the JPG: nothing is smoothed, sharpened or compressed again on the way. A JPG with a wide colour profile, such as the Display P3 of iPhone photos, is converted to sRGB, which tones down colours more vivid than sRGB can show. ## It cannot undo JPG compression When the JPG was saved, its encoder threw away fine detail and left small artefacts, such as blotchy patches in smooth areas and halos beside sharp edges. Those losses are baked into the pixels, and PNG preserves them faithfully: the result is no sharper and no cleaner than the JPG, with no hidden detail brought back. Getting detail back would take an enhancement tool that invents plausible texture, which is a different job altogether. ## The file will grow JPG reaches small sizes by being lossy, while PNG's compression is lossless and works best on flat colour, not on the fine grain of a photograph. Measured on a set of sample photos, a JPG saved at 90% turned into a PNG seven to eight times its size. The file list shows both sizes and the increase in percent. A chart or logo that was saved as JPG by mistake grows much less, and can even come out smaller. ## When PNG is still worth it - **Editing in several rounds.** Every time a JPG is saved it is compressed again, and the losses add up. Convert once, edit and save the PNG as often as you like, then export a single JPG at the end. - **Adding transparency later.** Removing a background or cutting out an object needs an alpha channel, which JPG lacks. The PNG made here is still fully opaque, but an editor can now erase parts of it and save the result. - **Sites that demand PNG.** Some uploads accept nothing else. Google Play, for example, wants an app's store icon as a 32-bit PNG of 512 × 512 pixels. The [image resizer](/resize-image/) sets an exact size such as 512 × 512. If none of these apply, the JPG is the better file to keep and share. For the opposite direction see [PNG to JPG](/png-to-jpg/), and for other formats the [image converter](/image-converter/). ## Camera details Photos carry EXIF data: camera model, date and often a GPS position. It is left out of the PNG by default. Choose *Keep, without location* to copy the EXIF data without the GPS position: camera and lens, exposure, date and time zone, and the photographer's name, copyright notice and camera serial number if the photo has them. PNG stores them in a chunk called eXIf. faq: - q: Does converting JPG to PNG improve image quality? a: No. The PNG keeps the picture as it is now, including any blurring or blockiness.
