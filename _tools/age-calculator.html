In the years between, the law decides when a leap-day baby is a year older, and it differs from place to place. The Age of Legal Capacity (Scotland) Act 1991 and Hong Kong's Age of Majority (Related Provisions) Ordinance both take the anniversary to be 1 March. New Zealand's driver licensing rule deems it to be 28 February. The calculator uses 1 March until you choose 28 February in the menu that appears for a 29 February birth. ## Chronological age for test scoring Chronological age is the time since birth, as opposed to a mental or developmental age. Schools, speech therapists and psychologists score a test against the child's chronological age on the test date, written in years, months and days. Test manuals have their own way to get it: subtract the birth date from the test date column by column, borrowing 12 months for a year and always 30 days for a month, and never round up. In the words of Pearson's Vineland manual, "all months are assumed to have 30 days". The answer can differ from the calendar by a day or two: born 20 September 2003 and tested 18 September 2013 is 9 years, 11 months, 28 days by the manual method and 29 days by the calendar. The big result is the calendar count and the list under it has the manual method, so use the one your manual asks for. ## Why some countries count age differently In the traditional East Asian count a baby is one year old at birth and everyone gains a year on New Year's Day, not on the birthday. A child born on 31 December was two the next morning. This "Korean age" is the current year minus the birth year, plus one, which is one or two years more than the international age shown here. South Korea made the international age its legal standard on 28 June 2023, although a few rules, such as the age for buying alcohol, still go by the year of birth. faq: - q: How old am I in days? a: The Days box under the result shows it. It counts every day from the date of birth to today, leap days included, but not the day of birth itself. As a guide, a 30th birthday falls on day 10,957 or 10,958, and day 10,000 comes at about 27 years and four and a half months. - q: Do you reach an age on your birthday or the day before? a: On the birthday itself in everyday use and in most modern law. In England and Wales the Family Law Reform Act 1969 fixed it at the start of the birthday. The older common-law rule, the day before, survives in places. The US Social Security Administration treats a person as reaching an age on the day before the birthday, so someone born on 1 January 1962 reaches 65 on 31 December 2026. - q: What day of the week was I born on? a: The list under the result names it. Dates are read in the Gregorian calendar used today. For a date before a country adopted it (1582 in much of Catholic Europe, September 1752 in Britain and its American colonies), convert the date first or the weekday will be wrong. - q: How do I calculate age in Excel or Google Sheets? a: With the date of birth in A2, `=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")` gives the full years, `"YM"` the months after them and `"MD"` the days after those. Microsoft advises against `"MD"` because of known limitations, so check a surprising result against this page. - q: Is my date of birth sent or stored anywhere? a: No. The age is worked out by JavaScript in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, saved or put in a cookie. The date goes into a link only when you press Copy link, so that you can bookmark or share a result. ---
