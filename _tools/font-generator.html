Unicode, the standard every phone and computer uses for text, has extra copies of the Latin alphabet: the Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block, added in Unicode 3.1 in 2001, holds bold, italic, script, Fraktur, double-struck, sans-serif and monospace letters for mathematics, and other blocks hold letters in circles and squares, small capitals from phonetic alphabets and full-width letters made for East Asian text. The generator swaps each letter for its copy. Usernames (handles) do not: Instagram and TikTok allow only plain letters, digits, full stops and underscores, X only A to Z, 0 to 9 and underscores, and Discord only a to z, 0 to 9, full stops and underscores. On X most styled letters count as two of your 280 characters. Discord and WhatsApp have real formatting instead: `**bold**`, `*italic*` and `~~strikethrough~~` in Discord, `*bold*`, `_italic_` and `~strikethrough~` in WhatsApp. For dates that show in each reader's time zone on Discord, use the [Discord timestamp generator](/discord-timestamp-generator/). ## Why some letters show as boxes The person reading sees your text in the fonts of their own device. When none of them has a character, it is drawn as an empty box or a question mark. Newer characters are the usual cause: the small capital ꞯ was added to Unicode in 2018 (version 11.0) and the superscript 𐞥 in 2021 (version 14.0), so older phones may not show them. Each device also draws the characters in its own way, which is why a style can look thinner, wider or more slanted on an iPhone than on an Android phone or a computer. ## Fancy text and screen readers Unicode Technical Report #25 says these letters are "intended for use only in mathematical or technical notation". In Adrian Roselli's tests of March 2025, NVDA, VoiceOver and Narrator did not read fake bold and italic words at all, JAWS spelled them out letter by letter with their style, and only Android's TalkBack read them as ordinary words. Search boxes and spelling checkers may not recognise them either.
