JPG encodes the picture in 8 × 8 pixel blocks and, in Chrome and Edge below 100%, stores colour at half resolution, so hard edges pick up faint ripples and coloured lettering looks smudged. The saving on such images is small too: in one test, a screenshot full of text was only 13% smaller at 90%. If a form insists on JPG for a screenshot, set the quality to 100, which in Chrome and Edge keeps colour at full resolution. The file grows, but the text stays crisp. ## Transparent PNGs A JPG cannot be see-through, so transparent pixels are painted with the colour under *Fill transparent areas with*. Partly transparent pixels, such as the smoothed rim of a logo or a soft drop shadow, are mixed with that colour. A logo flattened onto white and then placed on a dark background therefore shows a light outline. Choose the background colour of the document or page where the image will appear; each file's note names the colour that was used. ## Going the other way Turning a JPG back into PNG does not restore what JPG removed, so keep your original PNG files.
