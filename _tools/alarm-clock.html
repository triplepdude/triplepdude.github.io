Runs in your browser. category: Date & Time keywords: [online alarm clock, alarm clock, alarm clock online, set an alarm, alarm with snooze, wake up alarm, alarm clock with sound, night clock] howto: | ## How to set an alarm Type a time and press **Set alarm** or Enter. Write it as you would say it: 7:30, 7:30 pm, 19:30, 7.30, 7h30, 730, noon and midnight all work. The line under the alarm shows when it rings and how long that is from now, such as "Rings at 7:00 AM tomorrow (Monday), in 8 h 12 min". The quick alarms from 6:00 to 8:30 set one in a single click. To choose a wake-up time by sleep cycles, use the [sleep calculator](/sleep-calculator/). **Add an alarm** for each time you need, up to 10, with a label such as "Gym". An alarm rings once: **Snooze** brings it back after 5, 9, 10 or 15 minutes and **Stop** turns it off; one that nobody stops rings for 10 minutes. Bookmark an alarm to set it again each evening: `/alarm-clock/#6:30` sets a 6:30 alarm when you open it, and `#6:30~Gym,7:15` sets two. The address also keeps your sound and snooze choices. | Key | What it does | |---|---| | S or Space | Snooze a ringing alarm | | Esc | Stop a ringing alarm; leave full screen | | F | Full screen on or off | | N | Night mode on or off | ## Will it ring if my laptop sleeps? No. A web page cannot wake a sleeping computer, and closing a laptop's lid puts it to sleep, so leave the lid open, plug in the charger and set the computer not to sleep. While an alarm is set, the page asks to keep the screen on, and **Night mode** dims it to almost black. If the computer slept anyway, the page shows "Missed at 7:00 AM" when it wakes instead of ringing late. Microsoft says the same of the Windows Clock app: the computer has to stay awake. So for an alarm you cannot miss, use your phone's Clock app or an alarm clock. ## Can I switch tabs or minimise the browser? Yes. Chrome slows down the timers of hidden pages, so the check for the alarm is started straight from your click, and in its last minutes the sound is scheduled ahead on the sound card's clock, which keeps time in a background tab. To save memory, Chrome's Memory Saver may discard tabs you are not using, though never pinned tabs or the selected tab of a window. Edge puts background tabs to sleep after two hours, or after 5 to 30 minutes when it saves energy on battery, pinned or not. So give the alarm its own window, and add this site under "Always keep these sites active" (Chrome: Settings > Performance; Edge: Settings > System and performance > Performance). ## Does it work on a phone, or in silent mode? Only with the page in front and the screen on. Safari on an iPhone pauses the page once the screen locks or you switch apps, and Chrome on Android freezes a hidden tab after 5 minutes. Plug the phone in and use Night mode; Chrome on Android also vibrates while the alarm rings. In silent mode an iPhone mutes the sound that pages make themselves. While it rings or plays a test, this page asks Safari to treat its sound as media playback, which silent mode does not mute on iOS 17 and later, but check it with **Test sound** and the switch on before you rely on it. ## Can I wake up to my own song? Yes: choose a sound file, such as a song in MP3 or WAV, of up to 10 MB. The first 2 minutes play in a loop. AAC (.m4a) files play in most browsers but not all. The file never leaves your device and is not saved either, so after a reload the alarm rings with Bell until you choose it again. Press **Test sound** to check the volume, or try the [speaker test](/speaker-test/). For an alarm in 30 minutes rather than at a time, use the [online timer](/online-timer/#30m); for work sessions with breaks, the [focus timer](/pomodoro-timer/). faq: - q: How do I make sure I hear the alarm? a: "Press Test sound with the volume set as it will be at night: the computer's own volume and mute count too, and the sound plays through the speakers or headphones in use, so unplug headphones before you go to sleep. Do not mute the tab. The ready check under the settings shows whether the sound is on, the screen is kept on and the charger is plugged in." - q: Is anything uploaded or saved? a: "No. The alarms run in your browser. Their times, labels and settings are kept only in the page address after the # sign, which browsers never send to a server, so a reload keeps them; nothing goes into cookies or browser storage. Once loaded, the page needs no connection, so Wi-Fi that drops overnight does not stop it. A sound file you choose stays in the page's memory and is gone when the page closes." - q: What happens if I close the tab by mistake? a: A closed page cannot ring. Reopen it with Ctrl+Shift+T (Cmd+Shift+T on a Mac) and the alarms come back from the page address, still set; one whose time passed while the tab was closed shows that it was missed. A bookmark of the page sets its alarms again for their next time. - q: Why is snooze 9 minutes? a: Nobody knows for sure. The popular story that the gears of 1950s clocks could not count to ten is not true, and early snooze clocks used other lengths, such as the five or ten minutes of the 1959 Westclox Drowse. Nine minutes probably spread from General Electric's popular digital clock radios of the 1980s; the iPhone uses it too unless you change it, which iOS 26 allows. - q: Is snoozing bad for you? a: Not necessarily. In a 2023 study of 31 people who usually snooze, 30 minutes of snoozing cost them about 6 minutes of sleep and did not harm their thinking, mood or sleepiness after waking (Sundelin and colleagues, Journal of Sleep Research). If you snooze because you are tired, more sleep helps more; the CDC advises adults up to 60 to sleep 7 or more hours a night, and the NHS around 7 to 9. ---
