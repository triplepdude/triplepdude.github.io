Find the calculator that matches your question and type the two numbers: the answer, the formula and each step appear as you type. | Question | Formula | Example | |---|---|---| | What is X% of Y? | X ÷ 100 × Y | 15% of 80 = 12 | | X is what percent of Y? | X ÷ Y × 100 | 12 is 15% of 80 | | Change from X to Y | (Y − X) ÷ X × 100 | 80 to 100 = 25% increase | | X plus or minus Y% | X ± X × Y ÷ 100 | 80 + 15% = 92 | | X is Y% of what? | X ÷ (Y ÷ 100) | 12 is 15% of 80 | To find a percentage of a number, divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number: 15% of 80 is 0.15 × 80 = 12. To find what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100: 12 out of 80 is 12 ÷ 80 × 100 = 15%. For marks over several papers, divide the total you scored by the total available: [180 out of 240](#what=180,240) is 75%. ## Percentage increase and decrease Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. From 80 to 100 the change is 20, and 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%. Going back from 100 to 80, the same 20 is only a 20% decrease, because the starting value is now 100. For the same reason a 50% rise followed by a 50% fall leaves 75% of what you began with, not 100%. **Percentage difference** is for two values where neither one is the "before", such as two prices or two measurements. The gap is divided by the average of the two, so 80 and 100 differ by 20 ÷ 90 × 100 ≈ 22.22%, whichever you enter first. ## Reverse percentages: finding the original value To undo a percentage change, divide; do not take the same percentage off again. A price of 92 after a 15% rise was 92 ÷ 1.15 = 80. Taking 15% off 92 gives 78.20, which is wrong, because the 15% was worked out on the smaller original. After a 15% cut, divide by 0.85 instead: 68 ÷ 0.85 = 80. ## Discounts, VAT and tips Each of these examples opens in the calculator: - [20% off a price of 45](#subtract=45,20): 45 − 20% = 36. - [Add 20% VAT to 150](#add=150,20): 150 + 20% = 180. - [Take 20% VAT off 180](#before-increase=180,20): 180 ÷ 1.2 = 150. Each step shows how the number was read. faq: - q: What is the difference between a percentage change and percentage points? a: A percentage point is the plain difference between two percentages. If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it has risen by 1 percentage point, but by 25% as a percentage change, because 1 is a quarter of 4. Enter 4 and 5 in the increase or decrease calculator to get the 25%. - q: Is X% of Y the same as Y% of X? a: Yes. Both are X × Y ÷ 100, so 8% of 50 and 50% of 8 are both 4. Swapping the numbers is a useful shortcut when one of them makes an easy percentage, such as 50%, 25% or 10%. - q: Can a percentage be more than 100%? a: Yes, when the part is bigger than the whole, or when a value more than doubles. 150 is 150% of 100, and going from 50 to 150 is a 200% increase, which is the same as tripling. A decrease is never more than 100% unless the value ends up below zero. - q: How do I calculate a percentage in Excel or Google Sheets? a: "To see what percent A1 is of B1, type =A1/B1 and format the cell as a percentage. For a percentage change from A1 to B1 use =(B1-A1)/A1, and for 15% of A1 use =A1*15%. Spreadsheets store 15% as 0.15, so there is no need to multiply by 100." - q: Are the numbers I type sent anywhere? a: No. Every answer is worked out in your browser by the page itself.
