Choose, drop or paste an image: PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF, BMP, TIFF, HEIC or AVIF. A sample logo is traced until you do. 2. Say what it is: **Logo or drawing**, **Black and white** for cutting machines, or **Photo**. 3. Set the colours, speck size and smoothness. The image is traced again a moment after each change, in the background. 4. Drag the divider over the picture, or move the Compare slider, to check the trace against the original. Zoom in to see the edges. 5. **Download SVG**, or **Copy SVG code** to paste it into a web page. ## Choosing the settings | Setting | What it does | When to raise it | |---|---|---| | Colours | The most colours the trace may use; close shades are merged | The logo has more inks | | Speck size | Spots up to this many pixels across are dropped | Scans and JPGs | | Smoothness | How far curves may stray from the pixel edges | Round shapes, not small text | Each colour becomes one group of shapes in the SVG. The colours are stacked, each on top of the ones below it, so no hairline gaps show between shapes that touch. In the colour list you can change a colour or remove it; to drop a background, press **Pick a colour to remove** and click it. Transparent parts of a PNG stay transparent, and edge pixels that are at least half opaque count as solid. Big pictures are traced at up to 2 megapixels (photos at 0.5), and icons under 300 pixels are first enlarged 2 to 4 times, so their curves come out smooth. ## SVG cut files for Cricut and other cutting machines Choose **Black and white**. The page splits dark from light at the threshold that Otsu's method finds for your picture; move the slider to change it, or tick **Invert** to cut the light parts instead. Only the dark parts are drawn, as one black layer, and holes such as the middle of an O stay open. A logo traced in Logo mode gives one layer per colour: as the colours are stacked, each layer is a whole piece that the colours above it cover. Crop away what you do not need first with [Crop Image](/crop-image/). ## JPG to SVG, and other formats A JPG traces like a PNG, but its compression leaves speckles and soft edges, so raise the speck size. It has no transparency either: a white background is traced as a white shape until you remove it. For the other direction, the [SVG to PNG converter](/svg-to-png/) draws an SVG at any size, and the [image converter](/image-converter/) changes between bitmap formats. ## What vectorising can and cannot do A converter that traces, like this one, redraws the outline of each area of colour as lines and curves. The SVG then stays sharp at any size and can be cut, but it is a new drawing, not a lossless copy: fine detail, gradients and shadows become flat shapes, and text becomes outlines that can no longer be edited as text. Photos need many colours and thousands of shapes, so their SVG files are often larger than the JPG and slow to open. A PNG that is only wrapped in an SVG file is still a grid of pixels: it is not a vector, and a cutting machine cannot follow it. Tracing someone else's logo does not give you the right to use it. faq: - q: Can I convert a PNG to SVG without losing quality? a: Not exactly. Tracing redraws the picture as shapes, so flat logos and lettering come out close to the original and stay sharp at any size, while gradients, shadows and photo detail are simplified. An SVG that merely wraps the PNG keeps every pixel, but it is still a bitmap that cannot be scaled or cut cleanly. - q: Does the transparent background stay transparent? a: Yes. Transparent areas of a PNG are left empty in the SVG, and pixels that are at least half opaque count as part of a shape, so edges do not grow. A solid background colour can be taken out with Pick a colour to remove. - q: Why is my SVG blotchy or so large? a: Blotches come from JPG speckles, soft edges and more colours than the picture needs. Lower Colours, raise Speck size or Smoothness, or start from a larger, sharper image. The file grows with the number of shapes, which is why photos make big files; the page warns above 5,000 shapes. - q: Is my image uploaded to a server? a: No. The image is read and traced by your browser on this page, in a background thread, and the SVG is made there too. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is kept once you close the page. ---
