Rotate, flip and batch crop in your browser. category: Image & PDF keywords: [crop image, image cropper, crop image online, photo cropper, crop png, crop photo to 1080x1350, crop image to aspect ratio, batch crop images, crop jpg] howto: | ## How to crop an image 1. Choose, drop or paste a picture: JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF, GIF, TIFF, BMP, ICO or SVG. A sample is shown until you do. 2. Drag the box or one of its eight handles, or drag outside the box to draw a new one. Zoom with the mouse wheel, a two-finger pinch or the buttons. With the picture focused, the arrow keys move the box by one pixel (ten with Shift) and Alt with an arrow key resizes it. 3. Pick an aspect ratio or a ready-made size, or type the position and size of the box in pixels. 4. Choose the format, then **Download** or **Copy image**. Open several pictures at once to crop them all the same way into a ZIP file. ## Cropping to an exact size such as 1080 × 1350 Type the width and height, here 1080 and 1350, and move the box over the part you want: the file then holds exactly those pixels of your photo. To keep more of a large photo, pick **Instagram 1080 × 1350** under Ready-made size: the box keeps the 4:5 shape at any size and the crop is then scaled to that size. A smaller box is enlarged and looks soft; the page says so. ## Sizes for social media and prints Checked in November 2026. Platforms change these, so check their help pages too ([Instagram](https://help.instagram.com/1631821640426723), [YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431)). | Use | Pixels | Ratio | |---|---|---| | Instagram portrait post | 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 | | Instagram tall post | 1080 × 1440 | 3:4 | | Instagram square post | 1080 × 1080 | 1:1 | | Instagram landscape post | 1080 × 566 | 1.91:1 | | YouTube thumbnail | 1280 × 720 or 3840 × 2160 | 16:9 | | 4 × 6 in print at 300 dpi | 1800 × 1200 | 3:2 | | 5 × 7 in print at 300 dpi | 2100 × 1500 | 7:5 | | 8 × 10 in print at 300 dpi | 3000 × 2400 | 5:4 | Instagram keeps a photo's resolution, up to 1080 pixels wide, when its shape lies between 1.91:1 and 3:4, and since January 2025 the profile grid shows each post as a 3:4 thumbnail, which hides the sides of a 4:5 post. Scheduling apps that post through Meta's API stop at 4:5, so 1080 × 1350 is safer there. YouTube wants 16:9, at least 640 pixels wide, and suggests 3840 × 2160, but a thumbnail uploaded from a phone can be 2 MB at most. For prints, 300 pixels per inch is the usual target. The DPI field only records that number in a JPG, PNG, TIFF or BMP file, so a program that prints at the recorded size prints 1800 × 1200 pixels as 6 × 4 inches; the pixels stay the same. ## Does cropping lower the quality? The pixels inside the box are copied unchanged, but the file is saved again. PNG, TIFF, BMP and GIF are saved without loss. A JPG is compressed again at the quality estimated from the original, which loses a little more detail. (jpegtran can crop a JPG without compressing it again, but it moves the top-left corner onto the 8- or 16-pixel block grid; this page does not.) Colours are saved in sRGB, so the most saturated colours of a wide-gamut (Display P3) photo are slightly muted. Animated GIF, PNG and WebP files keep only their first frame, and camera RAW files are not supported: export a JPG or TIFF from your photo app first. A photo's rotation tag is applied first, so the crop is always upright. To straighten a tilted horizon, use [Flip Image](/flip-image/); for a round profile picture, [Crop Image to a Circle](/crop-image-circle/); to make a picture smaller without cutting anything off, the [image resizer](/resize-image/). ## Cropping on a phone Tap the box at the top to pick a photo from your library, iPhone HEIC photos included. Drag the box or a handle with one finger and pinch with two to zoom. Saved files go to Downloads on Android and to the Files app on an iPhone. faq: - q: Is my photo uploaded anywhere? a: No. Your pictures are opened, cropped and saved by your browser on this page. Nothing is sent to a server, and the page keeps no copy once you close it. - q: Can I crop several images the same way? a: Yes. Choose or drop them together. The box you set on the first picture is applied to every one at the same relative position, keeping the aspect ratio you picked, and you get one ZIP file with all the crops. - q: Why does my cropped image look blurry? a: The box probably covers fewer pixels than the output size, so the crop had to be enlarged. Make the box bigger, lower the output size, or choose Same as the crop. Zooming in on the editor shows the real pixels of your picture. - q: Does the cropped photo keep its location and date? a: The GPS location is always removed. The date, camera model and other EXIF details are kept when the crop is saved as JPG, PNG or WebP and the box to keep them is ticked; untick it to remove them too. Other formats are saved without them. ---
