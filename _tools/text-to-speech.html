--- title: "Text to Speech: Read Text Aloud Online Free" description: Paste text or open a .txt file and hear it read aloud by the voices in your browser, with word highlighting and speed, pitch and volume controls. category: Audio keywords: [text to speech, text to speech free, tts online, read aloud, read text out loud, text reader, text to voice, speech synthesis] howto: | ## How to read text aloud Type or paste text into the box, or open a .txt file, then press **Play**. The sentence being read is highlighted, and so is each word when the voice reports its position. Click any word to read on from there, or use **Previous** and **Next** to move one sentence at a time. Select part of the text before pressing Play to hear only that part. **Pause** stops at the current word and **Resume** carries on from it. A new voice, speed, pitch or volume takes effect straight away. ## Why the voices differ between devices The voices are not part of this page. Your browser offers the voices of your operating system, and some browsers add their own, which is why a phone and a laptop, or Chrome and Safari, list different ones. | Device | Where the voices come from | Adding voices | |---|---|---| | Windows | Windows; Chrome adds Google voices and Edge adds Microsoft's natural voices, both online | Settings > Time & language > Speech > Manage voices > Add voices | | Mac | macOS; Safari gives web pages no Siri voices and usually not the voices you download | System Settings > Accessibility > Read & Speak (Spoken Content in older versions), then the Info button next to System voice | | iPhone, iPad | iOS; browsers there give web pages no Siri voices and usually not the voices you download | Settings > Accessibility > Read & Speak or Spoken Content > Voices, though web pages usually cannot use the voices added there | | Android | The phone's text-to-speech engine | Settings > Accessibility > Text-to-speech output, then Install voice data in the engine's settings | Restart the browser after adding a voice. The list is grouped by language: type a name, a language or a code such as *fr* into **Find a voice** to narrow it. Pick a voice in the language of your text, because an English voice usually reads French words with English sounds and may skip a script it cannot read. The page warns you when the text is mostly in a script the chosen voice does not read, and names voices that do. ## Online voices A voice marked **Online** runs on a company's servers, not on your device. Google's voices in Chrome and the "Online (Natural)" voices in Edge are the common ones. To use one, your browser sends the text to Google or Microsoft over the internet, so it needs a connection. The page starts with a voice on your device when there is one, and shows a notice whenever an online voice is chosen. Voices without the label keep working offline once the page has loaded. Chrome's Google voices stop after about 15 seconds of speech, so for online voices the page sends shorter pieces, splitting long sentences at commas. ## Saving the speech as MP3 The browser plays the speech straight to your speakers and gives web pages no access to the sound, so a page that uses your browser's voices cannot save it as a file. To keep a copy, record your computer's sound output while the page reads. On Windows, Audacity can do that with its Windows WASAPI host and the loopback input of your speakers. ## How long the text can be The page reads up to 1,000,000 characters at a time, about 160,000 words of English. For a longer text, select a part of it and press Play. The Web Speech standard lets a browser limit one piece of speech to 32,767 characters; this page sends one sentence at a time, so that limit never applies. To read a photo or a scan aloud, take the text out with [image to text](/image-to-text/) first. If you hear nothing at all, check your output with the [speaker test](/speaker-test/). faq: - q: Is my text uploaded or saved anywhere? a: This page sends nothing and saves nothing, so there are no cookies and no stored copy of your text or settings. The one exception is a voice marked Online, for which your browser itself sends the text to Google or Microsoft to be spoken. For private text, keep to voices without that label. - q: Why does a voice stop after a sentence or two? a: This happens most often with Chrome's Google voices, which sometimes stop without telling the page that they have. When an online voice has not moved on for 20 seconds, the page says so. Press Next to carry on, or choose a voice without the Online label. - q: Does it work offline? a: Yes, with the voices without the Online label. They run on your device, and once the page has loaded it needs no connection. Voices marked Online need the internet, because your browser sends the text to Google or Microsoft to be spoken. - q: Why are no words highlighted with some voices? a: The word highlight follows position reports that the voice sends while it speaks, and some voices, including Google's voices in Chrome, send none. With those voices only the sentence is highlighted, and Resume starts again at the beginning of the sentence, or of the part being read when a long sentence is read in parts. - q: Why does a voice mispronounce names or read numbers oddly? a: Pronunciation comes from the voice, not from this page, so another voice may do better. You can also spell an unusual name the way it sounds, and write out dates, sums of money or abbreviations as you want them read. ---
