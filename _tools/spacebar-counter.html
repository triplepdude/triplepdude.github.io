--- title: Spacebar Counter and Spacebar Speed Test description: Count space bar presses freely or in a 5, 10, 30 or 60 second test, with presses per second, key-repeat timing and double-press checks, all in your browser. category: Device Tests keywords: [spacebar counter, spacebar test, space bar counter, spacebar speed test, spacebar clicker, spacebar counter 10 seconds, space bar test online, spacebar double typing test, key repeat rate test] howto: | ## How to use the spacebar counter Press the space bar and the number goes up. A press counts wherever you are on this page, except while another button, a link or the FAQ has the focus, and the page does not scroll while you press. On a phone or tablet, tap the large button instead: every tap counts, so two fingers can take turns. - **No limit** keeps counting until you press Reset, and shows how long you have been going. - **5, 10, 30 or 60 s** starts the clock with your first press and stops counting when the time runs out. A short pause follows, so a last hurried press does not start the next test. Links can open a timed test straight away: add `#10s` or `#60s` to the page address. ## How the numbers are worked out - **Per second** is presses divided by seconds: 64 presses in the 10-second test is 6.40. With no limit, the seconds run from your first press until now, so the average falls while you rest. - **Best second** is the most presses within any one second, wherever that second starts, so a burst that spans the clock's tick still counts in full. - **Shortest gap** is the least time between the start of one press and the start of the next. After a timed test, a bar for each second shows where you sped up or tired. ## Holding the bar down: key repeat Hold a key and your computer types it once, waits, then repeats it many times a second. Browsers mark those repeats, and the counter leaves them out, so a held bar is one press. Hold the space bar for about two seconds and the panel below the counter measures your keyboard's **repeat delay** (the wait before the first repeat) and **repeat rate**. | System | Where to change it | Range | |---|---|---| | Windows | Control Panel > Keyboard: Repeat delay, Repeat rate | About 250 ms to 1 s; about 2.5 to 30 repeats a second | | macOS | System Settings > Keyboard: Key repeat rate, Delay until repeat | Sliders without numbers | ## Double presses The counter flags every press of the bar that starts less than 30 ms after the one before it. That is over 33 presses a second, far beyond what a finger can do on one key, so it almost always means one press was read twice. Such presses are still counted, because the keyboard really sent them, but a score that includes them is not a fair one. To check other keys the same way, use the [keyboard tester](/keyboard-tester/), and to count mouse clicks rather than key presses, try the [CPS test](/cps-test/). faq: - q: How many times can you press the space bar in 10 seconds? a: About 50 with one finger. That is the average in the finger tapping test, where you tap a key with your index finger for 10 seconds; 183 adults aged 19 to 60 averaged 49.5 taps with their dominant hand (Heaton and others, 2020). Screens give much the same. A 2024 study of 370 adults found that those aged 50 to 70 tapped a phone or tablet 5.2 times a second, and that people aged 18 to 30 were faster with the index and middle fingers. - q: What is a good spacebar speed? a: Over 10 seconds, 5 presses a second is average for one finger and 6 or more is fast. In the tapping study above, the quickest of the 183 adults averaged 62.6 taps in 10 seconds. Taking turns with two fingers or both thumbs can push past what one finger manages. Rates fall as the hand tires, so expect fewer per second in the 60-second test than in the 10-second one. - q: Why does my space bar type two spaces? a: Usually because the switch under the bar bounces, so one press closes its contacts twice; this is called key chatter. Dirt under the bar and worn switches are the common causes, so clean underneath first, and replace a mechanical switch that still does it. Keyboards running QMK firmware let you raise the debounce time, 5 ms by default, and on Windows the Filter keys settings (Settings > Accessibility > Keyboard) can ignore unintended keystrokes until the fault is fixed. - q: Does the spacebar counter work on a phone? a: Yes. Tap the large button and each tap counts, including taps from two fingers in turn. The phone's on-screen keyboard is not used, because the page has no text box for it to type into, but a Bluetooth or USB keyboard works just as it does on a computer. - q: Are my presses recorded or sent anywhere? a: No. The page's own JavaScript counts each press and keeps the numbers in memory only. Nothing is uploaded or saved in your browser, and reloading the page clears every count. ---
