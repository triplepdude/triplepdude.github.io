--- title: "UUID Generator: v4, v7, v5, ULID & NanoID" description: Generate UUIDs (v4, v7, v1, v6 and name-based v3, v5, v8), ULIDs and NanoIDs in bulk, and decode any UUID or ULID to see its version and timestamp. category: Developer keywords: [uuid generator, uuid v4 generator, uuid v7 generator, uuid v5 generator, guid generator, ulid generator, nanoid generator, uuid decoder] howto: | ## How to use it Pick a type, choose how many you need (up to 1,000) and press **Generate**. For UUIDs, tick uppercase, no hyphens or braces; the same list is re-formatted rather than regenerated. **Copy** takes the whole list and **Download .txt** saves it one per line. Paste any UUID or ULID into **Decode** to see its version, variant, timestamp and the same 128 bits in the other format. ## Which UUID version? | Version | Built from | Sorts by time | |---|---|---| | 4 | 122 random bits | no | | 7 | Unix milliseconds + 74 random bits | yes | | 1 | 100 ns time since 1582 + clock sequence + node | no | | 6 | v1's fields reordered, time first | yes | | 3, 5 | MD5 or SHA-1 of a namespace and a name | no | | 8 | vendor-defined; here SHA-256 of namespace and name | no | Use **v4** when the ID must not reveal anything, and **v7** for database keys: new rows land at the end of the index. Random bits come from `crypto.getRandomValues`, the browser's secure generator. Within one millisecond, v7 treats its random bits as a counter and adds a random step (RFC 9562, section 6.2), so a batch sorts in the order it was made. **v1 and v6** here use a random node with the multicast bit set and a random clock sequence (RFC 9562, section 6.10), never your network card's MAC address. IDs made in the same millisecond take the next 100 ns tick. ## Name-based UUIDs (v3, v5, v8) The same namespace and name always give the same UUID, which is handy for turning URLs, e-mail addresses or keys from another system into stable IDs. Enter one name per line. Names are hashed as UTF-8 exactly as typed, so case and spaces matter. The standard namespaces are DNS (`6ba7b810-…`), URL (`6ba7b811-…`), OID (`6ba7b812-…`) and X.500 (`6ba7b814-…`); any UUID can serve as a custom one. Prefer **v5** (SHA-1) over v3 (MD5). The v8 option follows RFC 9562 Appendix B.2 and uses SHA-256: `www.example.com` in the DNS namespace gives `5c146b14-3c52-8afd-938a-375d0df1fbf6`. The **nil UUID** is all zeros, often meaning "no ID". The **max UUID** is all `f`, a sentinel such as "end of the list". ## ULID A ULID is 128 bits like a UUID: a 48-bit millisecond timestamp and 80 random bits, written as 26 characters of Crockford's Base32 (no I, L, O or U), such as `01ARZ3NDEKTSV4RRFFQ69G5FAV`. ULIDs sort by time as text. Within one millisecond each ULID is the previous one plus 1 (the monotonic mode of the spec). The decoder converts a ULID to UUID form for databases with a `uuid` column, and back. ## NanoID NanoID makes short random strings from any alphabet. The default, 21 URL-safe characters, carries 126 random bits, about as many as v4. Pick a preset or type your own characters. Characters are chosen by rejection sampling, so each is equally likely even when the alphabet size is not a power of two. The page shows the bits per ID and roughly how many IDs give a 1% chance of any two being equal. A **GUID** is Microsoft's name for a UUID. Windows stores the first three groups little-endian in binary, but the text form is the same. faq: - q: Can two generated UUIDs ever be the same? a: In theory yes, in practice no. A v4 UUID has 122 random bits, so you would need about 2.7 quintillion (2.7 × 1018) of them for a 50% chance of a single repeat. v7 IDs can only clash within the same millisecond, and within one batch from this page each is larger than the one before. - q: Why does my v5 UUID differ from another tool's? a: Check the namespace, and that the name matches byte for byte. `www.example.com` and `www.example.com ` (trailing space) or `WWW.example.com` give different UUIDs. Some tools also hash the name in another encoding than UTF-8. - q: Can I find out when a UUID or ULID was created? a: Yes for v1, v6, v7 and ULID, which store a time; paste one into the decoder. v4 and NanoID are pure randomness, and v3, v5 and v8 name-based IDs are hashes, so they contain no date. - q: Should I use UUID v7 or ULID? a: Both put a millisecond timestamp first. Use v7 when your database or language has a UUID type, and ULID when you want a shorter, case-insensitive string. The decoder converts one form into the other. - q: Are these IDs generated on a server? a: No. They are created in your browser with the Web Crypto API, and the decoder also runs locally. Nothing is uploaded, logged or stored. ---
