The default small margin (6 mm, about ¼ inch) keeps a scan's edge clear of the unprintable border most printers leave. ## Image quality and file size With compression **off**, JPEG photos are copied into the PDF without re-encoding. The PDF format stores JPEG data as it is (the `DCTDecode` filter of ISO 32000), so there is no quality loss. Only the compressed picture is copied: EXIF (GPS location, camera serial number), XMP, IPTC, comments and a motion photo's video are left out. The EXIF rotation tag is applied with the page's transformation matrix, and a colour profile such as the Display P3 of phone photos is kept. Other images are decoded by your browser and stored either as JPEG at the quality you set, or **lossless** with Flate compression and PNG row predictors, which keeps screenshots and scanned text crisp. HEIC and AVIF photos always become JPEG. Turn compression **on** to shrink the PDF. Every image is re-encoded as JPEG and scaled down to the resolution limit, in pixels per inch (ppi) at the size it is printed. 150 ppi looks sharp on screen and is fine for everyday printing; choose 300 ppi for high-quality prints. A photo that re-encoding would make bigger is kept as it was. The estimate above the Create PDF button shows the size before you save. SVG drawings are rendered at 300 ppi for the size they are printed at, so lines stay sharp. From a multi-page TIFF only the first page is used, from a HEIC file its main image, and from an animation its first frame. ## Transparency, title and author Transparent areas are normally flattened onto white, as they would look on paper. **Keep transparency** stores the alpha channel as a soft mask, so a logo or signature stays see-through when the page is placed over other artwork. The title and author are saved in the document properties, and PDF readers show that title in the window instead of the file name. The output is a standard PDF 1.7 file (ISO 32000-1). faq: - q: Are my images uploaded to a server? a: No. The images are read and the PDF is assembled by JavaScript running in this page, on your own device.
