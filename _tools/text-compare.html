--- title: "Text Compare: Online Diff Checker" description: Compare two texts and see every difference highlighted by line, word or character. Side-by-side or inline view, ignore whitespace or case, in your browser. category: Text keywords: [text compare, diff checker, compare text online, text difference checker, compare two texts, diff tool, find differences between two texts, compare documents] howto: | ## How to compare two texts Paste the original text on the left and the changed text on the right, or open a file on either side. The differences appear as you type. Removed text is marked with a minus sign and a red background, added text with a plus sign and a green background, so the marks still work if you can't tell the colours apart. **Swap** exchanges the two sides and **Download .diff** saves the changes as a patch file. ## Line, word or character? | Compare by | Best for | What is highlighted | |---|---|---| | Line | Code, lists, CSV, config files | Whole lines, with the changed words inside a changed line | | Word | Prose, contracts, articles | Individual words and punctuation, in running text | | Character | Short strings, IDs, typos | Single characters | **Side by side** shows the original and changed versions in two columns; **Inline** merges them into one column, which reads better on a phone. **Hide unchanged lines** folds long stretches that are identical and keeps three lines of context around each change. ## How the comparison works The tool uses Eugene Myers' O(ND) difference algorithm (1986), the same method behind GNU diff and Git's default diff. It finds the shortest edit script: the fewest insertions and deletions that turn one text into the other, which is the same as finding their longest common subsequence. It runs in linear memory by searching from both ends for the middle of the edit path, and lines that appear on only one side are set aside first, a speed-up GNU diff also uses. The work happens in a background thread, so the page stays responsive with texts of many thousands of lines. In word and character mode the texts are first compared line by line, then each changed block is compared again word by word or character by character. Words, runs of spaces and punctuation marks are separate tokens. Characters are grapheme clusters, so an emoji or an accented letter counts as one. ## Reading the numbers Within each block of changes, removed lines are paired with added lines in order. Each pair counts as one **changed** line and the rest as **added** or **removed**. If three lines are replaced by five, you see 3 changed and 2 added. Unchanged lines are the lines the two texts have in common. ## Ignoring whitespace and case With **Ignore whitespace**, any run of spaces or tabs counts as a single space and spaces at the start or end of a line don't count, so re-indented or re-spaced lines match. Line breaks still count. In word and character mode, added or removed spaces and tabs are not highlighted at all. **Ignore case** compares text as if it were all lower case. Both options affect only the comparison; the texts are shown as you entered them. ## Patch files The downloaded `.diff` file is a standard unified diff, the format of `diff -u` and `git diff`, with three lines of context. It is always made from the exact texts, even when an ignore option is on, so `patch original.txt < text-compare.diff` turns the original into the changed text. faq: - q: Is my text uploaded anywhere? a: No. Both texts are compared in your browser, in a background thread on your own device. Nothing is sent to a server or stored, and opened files are read locally. - q: Why is a line I edited shown as removed and added? a: A diff describes changes as removals and additions. When a removed line and an added line sit in the same place they are paired as a changed line, and the words that differ inside it are highlighted more strongly. - q: Do Windows and Unix line endings count as differences? a: No. Browsers store line breaks in a text box as a single line feed, and opened files are converted the same way, so CRLF and LF files compare as equal. - q: How large can the texts be? a: Several thousand lines compare almost instantly on a typical computer. Very large texts that differ almost everywhere take longer, but the page stays usable while the comparison runs. - q: Why do the differences look different from another diff tool? a: When several edits are equally short, tools can pick different ones, for example which of two identical blank lines is kept. Every minimal diff is correct; they just group the changes differently. ---
