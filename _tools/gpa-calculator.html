Add a semester for each term on your transcript. If your transcript already shows a GPA, enter it with the credits it covers under **Earlier semesters** instead of typing every old course again, and its weighted GPA too if it shows one. Choose **No grade** for pass/fail, withdrawn or audited courses, which usually leave the GPA alone. The page address keeps everything you enter, so a reload or a bookmark brings it back. ## How GPA is calculated Each grade is worth a number of points (A = 4.0, B+ = 3.3 and so on). Multiply the points by the course's credits, add up these grade points and divide by the total credits: | Course | Grade | Points | Credits | Grade points | |---|---|---|---|---| | Biology | A | 4.0 | 4 | 16.0 | | English | B+ | 3.3 | 3 | 9.9 | | Calculus | A− | 3.7 | 4 | 14.8 | | History | B | 3.0 | 3 | 9.0 | | **Total** | | | **14** | **49.7** | The GPA is 49.7 ÷ 14 = **3.55**. A four-credit course counts a third more than a three-credit one. If every class has the same credits, the GPA is simply the average of the points. GPAs are shown rounded to two decimals; the working line shows more digits, because schools differ in how they round. ## Weighted and unweighted GPA An unweighted GPA treats every class alike, so 4.0 is the top. A weighted GPA adds a bonus for harder classes. A common scheme adds 0.5 for Honors and 1.0 for AP, IB and dual-enrolment classes, so an A in AP counts 5.0; change the bonuses to match your school. An F stays at 0 in every type of class. In the example above, Honors English and AP Calculus raise the weighted GPA to 55.2 ÷ 14 = **3.94**. Colleges may recalculate your GPA with their own rules, adjusting or removing the weighting. ## Cumulative GPA Your cumulative GPA combines every semester by credits, not by averaging the semester GPAs. With 3.20 over 60 earlier credits and 3.80 over 15 new ones: (3.20 × 60 + 3.80 × 15) ÷ 75 = 249 ÷ 75 = **3.32**. ## How to raise your GPA The target planner solves for the average you need from now on: `needed = (target × all credits − grade points so far) ÷ credits left` To go from 3.32 over 75 credits to 3.50 with 30 credits left, you need (3.50 × 105 − 249) ÷ 30 = **3.95**, almost straight A's. The more credits you have, the less each new grade moves the result, so plan early. Ask whether your school replaces the grade when you retake a class: that can help more than any new A. To see what you need on one exam, use the [final grade calculator](/final-grade-calculator/); to work out a class grade from your marks, use the [grade calculator](/grade-calculator/). ## All A's and one B With classes of equal credits, all A's and one B gives 4 − 1 ÷ (number of classes): 3.75 with 4 classes, 3.80 with 5, 3.83 with 6 and 3.86 with 7. faq: - q: What is a good GPA? a: US high school graduates averaged 3.11 on a 4.0 scale in 2019, up from 3.00 in 2009, according to the NAEP High School Transcript Study. Above 3.5 is well above that average. What counts as good depends on the colleges, scholarships or programmes you want, so look up the GPA ranges they publish. - q: What letter grade is a 3.5 GPA? a: On the standard 4.0 scale a 3.5 sits halfway between a B+ (3.3) and an A− (3.7). Equal-credit classes with half A− and half B+ grades give exactly 3.5, and so do half A's and half B's. - q: How do I turn a percentage into a GPA? a: Find the letter for your percentage in the scale table, then its points. On the common US scale, 91% is an A− and 3.7 points, and 85% is a B and 3.0 points. Your school's own cut-offs on your report card or syllabus always win. - q: Are my grades uploaded or saved? a: "No. The calculator runs in your browser, sends nothing to a server and saves nothing in your browser's storage. Your courses are kept only in the page address after the # sign, which browsers never send to the site; like any address, it stays in your browser history until you clear it." ---
