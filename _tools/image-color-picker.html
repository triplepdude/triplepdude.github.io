Choose an image, drop it on the page or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V (⌘V on a Mac). JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF, GIF, TIFF, BMP and SVG all open. A sample picture is shown until you do. 2. Move over the picture: the magnifier shows the pixels around the pointer, enlarged, with the one you will pick outlined. Click to pick it. On a phone, tap a pixel to pick it, or touch and hold until the magnifier appears above your finger, slide to the right pixel and lift; a swipe scrolls the page. 3. Copy the code you need. Every pick is added to **Picked colours**, newest first. With the keyboard, Tab to the picture, move one pixel with the arrow keys (ten with Shift) and press Enter or Space to pick. ## HEX, RGB, HSL, HSB and CMYK | Format | Example | The numbers are | |---|---|---| | HEX | #1F6F4A | red, green and blue, two hexadecimal digits each | | RGB | rgb(31, 111, 74) | the same three amounts from 0 to 255 | | HSL | hsl(152, 56%, 28%) | hue angle, saturation, lightness | | HSB (HSV) | 152°, 72%, 44% | hue, saturation, brightness, as in Photoshop's picker | | CMYK | cmyk(72%, 0%, 33%, 56%) | cyan, magenta, yellow and black ink | {: .data} HEX and RGB are the same colour written in two number bases: 1F is 1 × 16 + 15 = 31. CSS accepts HEX, RGB and HSL as they are shown. A partly transparent pixel also gets its opacity, as in #FF000080 or rgba(255, 0, 0, 0.5). The CMYK figures use the plain formula K = 1 − max(R, G, B) ÷ 255, which knows nothing about ink or paper; printers convert with a profile for their press, so ask them for exact values. To convert a code you already have, or to check its contrast with white and black text, use the [HEX to RGB converter](/hex-to-rgb/). ## One pixel or an average Photos are noisy: neighbouring pixels of a blue sky can differ by several steps. **3 × 3 average** and **5 × 5 average** take the mean of the 9 or 25 pixels around the one you pick, like the sample sizes of Photoshop's eyedropper, and give the colour you see rather than one stray pixel. Keep **Single pixel** for logos, screenshots and flat graphics. ## Getting a palette from a photo The palette lists the 5 to 10 colours that best describe the image, largest share first. The tool compares colours in the OKLab colour space, where distances follow how different colours look. It keeps splitting the image's colours where a split separates them most (variance-based median cut), refines the groups with k-means, and shows each group's average colour and its share of the pixels. Pixels that are more than half transparent are left out, images over 262,144 pixels are sampled on an even grid, and when a photo has more than 32,768 different colours, very close shades are grouped first. ## Why the values can differ from Photoshop In Chrome and Edge the codes here are sRGB values: the browser applies the colour profile stored in the file, as it does to show the image, and sRGB is what CSS needs. Photoshop shows the numbers stored in the file, in the file's own profile such as Display P3 or Adobe RGB, so one pixel can read 200, 100, 50 there and 215, 93, 31 here. When a JPEG, PNG or WebP file carries a profile, the tool names it and offers **As stored in the file** to read the stored numbers. Other causes: a JPEG saved again shifts colours slightly, a screenshot holds what your display showed, and a partly transparent pixel can come back slightly off, the more so the more transparent it is, because browsers store such pixels multiplied by their opacity. iPhone HEIC photos open here directly; to keep a copy in another format, use the [image converter](/image-converter/) or [HEIC to JPG](/heic-to-jpg/). faq: - q: Is my image uploaded anywhere? a: No. The image is opened and read by your own browser, on this page.
