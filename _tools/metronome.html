Subdivisions add quieter clicks between the beats: eighth notes, triplets or sixteenth notes. In 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 the sixteenths are quieter still, so the eighth notes stay clear. ## Tempo markings in BPM The table lists the ranges Wittner gives for its Taktell mechanical metronome, whose scale runs from 40 to 208: Andante is 76 to 108 BPM and Allegro 120 to 168. Other references draw the lines elsewhere (Wikipedia gives Allegro as 120 to 156), because the words describe character as much as speed. A metronome mark is exact: M.M. ♩ = 120 means 120 quarter notes a minute. M.M. stands for Mälzel's Metronome, after Johann Nepomuk Mälzel, who patented it in 1815. ## How to practise with a metronome Start at a tempo at which you play the passage without mistakes, even half the marked speed. Raise it by 2 to 5 BPM once it feels easy, and go back a step when mistakes creep in. Subdivisions show whether you rush between beats. For swing, set half the tempo, turn off **Accent beat 1** and hear the clicks as beats 2 and 4: at 80 BPM they fall on 2 and 4 of a bar at 160. Tune first with the [online tuner](/online-tuner/), or play a reference A with the [tone generator](/tone-generator/). ## Why this metronome does not drift A metronome that times each click with a JavaScript timer drifts and stutters: timers fire late while the page is busy with layout or garbage collection, browsers may run them only once a second in a background tab, and Chrome only once a minute in a tab that has been hidden for 5 minutes and silent for 30 seconds. Here a timer only fills a queue: every 25 ms it books the next clicks on the Web Audio clock, which runs on its own thread, so each click starts within one sample of its time (0.02 ms at 48 kHz). Clicks are booked 0.25 seconds ahead while the page is in view and 2 seconds ahead in a background tab, so a late timer changes nothing. faq: - q: Is anything recorded or uploaded? a: No. The clicks are generated by your browser's Web Audio engine, and the page never uses the microphone. Nothing is sent to a server or stored; your settings live only in the page address. - q: Does it keep playing in another tab or with the screen off? a: In another tab on a computer, yes, because the clicks are queued 2 seconds ahead while the page is hidden. Phones may pause a page when the screen locks or you switch apps, so while it plays the page asks the phone to keep the screen on. - q: Why is there no sound on my iPhone? a: Turn off silent mode, because iOS can mute sound from web pages while it is on. Then check the Volume slider here and your phone's volume. - q: Why does the click sound late through Bluetooth headphones? a: Bluetooth audio is buffered, so every click reaches you a fraction of a second after it is played. The delay is the same for every click, so the tempo stays exact, but to play along with other people or to record, use wired headphones or the speaker. ---
