South and west can be written with a minus sign instead of a letter. ## How an MGRS grid reference is built Take `18T WL 85631 11326`. **18T** is the grid zone: UTM zone 18 and latitude band T. Bands are 8° tall and lettered C (80°S) to X, which is 12° tall and runs from 72°N to 84°N; I and O are skipped. **WL** names a 100 km square: the column letter comes from the easting and the row letter from the northing, in cycles that repeat so that neighbouring zones use different letters. **85631 11326** are the easting and northing inside that square. | Digits | Precision | |---|---| | 10 (5 + 5) | 1 m | | 8 | 10 m | | 6 | 100 m | | 4 | 1 km | | 2 | 10 km | The digits are truncated, not rounded: a reference names the square the point lies in, measured from its south-west corner. That is why the last MGRS digit can be one lower than the rounded UTM value. When you convert an MGRS reference back to latitude and longitude, you get the centre of its square. ## UTM zones and their exceptions UTM splits the world into 60 zones, each 6° of longitude wide, numbered eastward from 180°W. Each zone is a transverse Mercator projection with a scale factor of 0.9996 on its central meridian, a false easting of 500,000 m, and a false northing of 10,000,000 m in the southern hemisphere. Two areas break the pattern, and the converter applies both: - **Norway:** zone 32V is widened to run from 3°E to 12°E between 56°N and 64°N, so zone 31V is only 3° wide there. - **Svalbard:** between 72°N and 84°N, zones 31X, 33X, 35X and 37X are widened to 9° or 12°, and zones 32X, 34X and 36X are not used. ## Accuracy and limits The math uses the WGS84 ellipsoid and Krüger's series for the transverse Mercator projection, the method used by GeographicLib and PROJ. Results agree with PROJ to well under a millimetre. MGRS and UTM cover 80°S to 84°N only; the polar regions use the Universal Polar Stereographic (UPS) grid with bands A, B, Y and Z, which this converter does not support. If your coordinates come from an old paper map, check its datum: NAD27, ED50 and other local datums put the same point tens to hundreds of metres away from WGS84. faq: - q: Is my location sent anywhere? a: No. All conversions run in your browser. "Use my location" asks your browser for your position, and it stays on your device; nothing is uploaded or stored. - q: What is the difference between MGRS and UTM? a: They are the same grid. UTM gives a zone number and two large numbers in metres. MGRS adds a latitude band letter and replaces the leading digits of the easting and northing with two letters for the 100 km square, which makes references shorter and harder to misread. - q: Why do my last digits differ from another app? a: MGRS digits are truncated to the square the point lies in, while UTM values are usually rounded, so they can differ by one in the last place. Apps that round MGRS, or use a datum other than WGS84, can also give slightly different digits. - q: Can I convert coordinates near the North or South Pole? a: Not here. MGRS north of 84°N and south of 80°S uses the Universal Polar Stereographic grid, which this converter does not handle. It tells you when a point falls in that area. ---
