Runs in your browser. category: Device Tests keywords: [typing test, typing speed test, wpm test, words per minute test, 1 minute typing test, 5 minute typing test, typing accuracy test, typing test with passages, free typing test] howto: | ## How to take the typing test Choose a length and a text, click or tap the text box and type what it shows. The clock starts with your first key and stops when the time is up or you reach the end of the passage. Space moves on to the next word, and Backspace can take you back into earlier words to fix them. Esc starts a new test at once; so does Tab and then Enter, because Tab moves to the Restart button. The text still to type is grey, and letters you type correctly take the normal text colour. A wrong letter turns red with a wavy underline, extra letters appear in red, and letters you skip get a dotted underline. Curly quotation marks, which phone keyboards often type, count as straight ones. ## How WPM, accuracy and consistency are worked out Typing speed counts a *word* as any five characters, spaces and punctuation included, so long and short words weigh the same. | Score | How it is counted | |---|---| | WPM | Characters of the words typed correctly, plus the space after each, ÷ 5 ÷ minutes | | Raw WPM | Every character you typed and kept, right or wrong, ÷ 5 ÷ minutes | | Accuracy | Keys that typed the expected character ÷ all character keys, mistakes you fixed included | | Consistency | 100% minus the coefficient of variation (standard deviation ÷ mean) of the characters typed in each second, deleted ones included | For example, you type 330 characters in the 1-minute test and 310 of them are in words without a mistake: your WPM is 310 ÷ 5 = 62 and your raw WPM 66. A word left wrong adds nothing to WPM. ## What is a good typing speed? In an online study of 168,000 volunteers copying English sentences (Dhakal and others, CHI 2018), the average was 51.6 WPM: | Speed | Where it put a typist | |---|---| | Under about 26 WPM | Slowest 10% | | About 52 WPM | Average | | Over about 78 WPM | Fastest 10% | | 120 WPM or more | The very fastest | The study counted the whole text typed, so compare it with raw WPM. On phones, a study of 37,370 volunteers (Palin and others, 2019) measured 36.2 WPM on average. ## Where the passages come from A book passage starts at the beginning of a chapter of one of these books, all out of copyright in the UK and the US. If a key misses or types twice, check it with the [keyboard tester](/keyboard-tester/). To see how long a document would take you to type, divide its characters, counted by the [word counter](/word-counter/), by 5 and by your WPM: 6,000 characters at 40 WPM take 30 minutes. faq: - q: Is 40 WPM good enough for a job? a: It is a common minimum for office and clerical jobs. The State of California, for example, accepts a proctored five-minute typing test with at least 40 words per minute. To practise for a test like that, choose 5 minutes and a book passage, which has capitals and punctuation, and aim for few errors as well as speed. - q: Why is accuracy more important than speed? a: A mistake costs time twice, once to type it and again to notice, delete and retype it. In the 2018 study of 168,000 typists, people who corrected fewer mistakes were generally faster. Slow down until your accuracy stays above about 95%, then push for speed. - q: Can I take the typing test on a phone or tablet? a: Yes. Tap the text to open the on-screen keyboard; swipe typing and predictive text work too, because the test reads what reaches the text box, but text added in one go from the keyboard's clipboard is not counted. If auto-correct changes your words, turn it off. Pressing Space twice types a full stop on most phones, which counts as an error. On an iPhone, switch off "." Shortcut under Settings, General, Keyboard; in Gboard on Android, switch off Double-space period under Text correction. - q: Why do other typing tests give me a different score? a: Sites count differently. Some count every character you type, as raw WPM does here; some subtract one word per minute for each error left in the text; others, like this page, count only the words typed correctly. Lower-case common words are also quicker to type than book text with capitals and punctuation, so compare scores from the same kind of test. - q: Is anything I type saved or sent anywhere? a: No. The test runs in your browser and keeps results in memory only, so they are gone when you reload or close the tab. The word list and passages are files on this site, and nothing you type is uploaded or stored. ---
