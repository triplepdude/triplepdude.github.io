Switch the numbering to US if your calendar starts on Sunday. ## How ISO 8601 week numbers work ISO 8601 defines three rules. Weeks start on Monday. Week 1 is the week that contains the first Thursday of the year, which is the same as the week that contains January 4. And every week belongs entirely to the year that holds its Thursday, called the *week-year*. Because of that last rule, the days around New Year can belong to a neighbouring year's week. December 29 to 31 can fall in week 1 of the next year, and January 1 to 3 can fall in week 52 or 53 of the previous one. Always pair an ISO week with its week-year, not the calendar year. | Date | Weekday | ISO week | US week | |---|---|---|---| | 2020-12-31 | Thursday | 2020-W53 | 53 | | 2021-01-03 | Sunday | 2020-W53 | 2 | | 2024-12-30 | Monday | 2025-W01 | 53 | | 2027-01-01 | Friday | 2026-W53 | 1 | ## Years with 53 weeks Most years have 52 ISO weeks. A year has 53 when January 1 is a Thursday, or when it is a leap year that starts on a Wednesday. That happens in 71 of every 400 years; 2020 and 2026 are recent examples, and the next one after 2026 is 2032. December 28 is always in the last week of its year, which gives a quick way to count them. ## US week numbers (not ISO) Many US calendars count weeks from Sunday and call the week containing January 1 week 1, however few days of it fall in the new year. The first and last weeks are often partial, so a year has 53 weeks and occasionally 54 (2028 does, because it is a leap year that starts on a Saturday). This is what `WEEKNUM(date)` returns in Excel and Google Sheets. The ISO number comes from `ISOWEEKNUM(date)` instead. ## Getting ISO weeks in code - **Python:** `date.isocalendar()` returns the week-year, week and weekday. - **strftime:** use `%G-W%V`. Pairing `%V` with `%Y` gives the wrong year at the turn of the year, and `%U` is a Sunday-based count that starts at week 0. - **PostgreSQL:** `EXTRACT(WEEK FROM d)` and `EXTRACT(ISOYEAR FROM d)`. - **MySQL:** `WEEK(d, 3)` is the ISO week. This page does its date math on plain year, month and day numbers rather than on clock times, so daylight saving changes can never shift a date into the wrong week. faq: - q: What week number is it right now? a: The number at the top of this page is the current ISO 8601 week, calculated from your device's date. If January 1 falls on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday, that week's Thursday is in December, so the first days of January count as the last week of the previous year. - q: How many weeks are in a year? a: A year has 52 ISO weeks, or 53 when it starts on a Thursday (or on a Wednesday in a leap year). They are the European standard and the default in most planning and ERP software. Use the US system only when your calendar or spreadsheet already counts weeks from Sunday. - q: Is anything I enter sent to a server? a: No. Every calculation runs in your browser with JavaScript.
